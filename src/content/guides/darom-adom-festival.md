Each year, between late January and late February, the Negev desert in southern Israel performs one of the Mediterranean world's most striking natural spectacles: the kalaniot bloom. Hillsides, roadsides, and fields in the Eshkol region fill with carpets of red Anemone coronaria — Israel's national flower — until the landscape looks more like a painting than a desert.

The Darom Adom festival (Hebrew: "South Red") is the annual celebration organised by the Eshkol Regional Council to welcome visitors to the bloom. It's a distinctly Israeli institution: part wildflower walk, part Bedouin hospitality tent, part mountain-bike marathon, part outdoor concert — and entirely specific to this corner of the northern Negev.

---

## What is Darom Adom?

Darom Adom is not a fixed-venue music or arts festival — it is a landscape-scale event built around a natural phenomenon. The bloom itself is the centrepiece; the festival activities are organised around it.

The festival is run by the **Eshkol Regional Council**, which manages the rural agricultural and nature areas where the anemones grow most densely. In practical terms, Darom Adom means:

- **Marked bloom trails**: walking routes through the most flower-dense fields, updated each year to follow the bloom
- **Mountain-bike marathons**: routes weaving through wildflower areas in the Negev landscape
- **Live concerts**: Israeli musicians perform at outdoor stages within the bloom areas
- **Bedouin hospitality tents**: traditional tea, dates, and local food in tent settings within the fields
- **Agricultural tours**: working moshav (cooperative village) visits alongside the bloom
- **Shuttle buses**: on peak weekends, shuttles connect car parks to the main bloom sites to reduce road congestion

Entry to the outdoor areas and walking trails is free in most cases. Some ticketed evening events carry a nominal charge. The festival's scope and programme vary by year depending on bloom intensity.

---

## When to visit

Darom Adom's dates are not fixed in advance. The festival is announced approximately **2–4 weeks before the bloom peaks**, once conditions are confirmed. In most years:

- **Mid-January**: first scattered anemones appear in coastal and southern areas; full bloom not yet reached
- **Late January to mid-February**: bloom building in the Eshkol region; some years produce early carpet-effect blooms in warm, high-rainfall winters
- **Mid-to-late February**: the most likely peak week for the dramatic carpet-covering bloom
- **Early March**: bloom still present in some areas after a late or slow-developing season

The variability is real. In a drought year, even peak February can produce only scattered flowers rather than the full carpet effect. In a very good rainfall year, the bloom can be extraordinary.

**Before booking travel specifically for the bloom:** Check [daromadom.co.il](https://www.daromadom.co.il) for current bloom status updates, which are published weekly from January. The Israel Nature and Parks Authority also tracks wildflower conditions at [inpa.gov.il/flowers](https://www.inpa.gov.il). Never make the drive based on last year's dates — conditions change every season.

## Best bloom locations

The bloom concentrates in the **Eshkol region** of the northern Negev, roughly 25–40 km west and south-west of Beer Sheva. Key sites include:

**Shokeda Forest**: a JNF-planted forest whose inter-tree areas turn into dense red carpets. The contrast between the dark pine plantation and the red anemone floor makes this one of the most photographed Darom Adom sites. Accessible by car via Route 2357.

**Moshav Patish**: kalaniot fields alongside working farmland in the agricultural moshav. The combination of cultivated fields and wildflower bloom in a working farming landscape is distinctive.

**Ruhama Badlands and Halutza Sands**: the loess hills and sandy terrain around Kibbutz Ruhama produce a different bloom pattern — anemones spread across open ground rather than between forest cover, often in very high density in good years.

**Coastal plain sites**: anemones also bloom in the coastal plain north of Ashdod (easier to reach without a car), but the density is typically lower than in the Eshkol region.

The specific "best field" changes year to year as bloom progresses across the landscape. The daromadom.co.il bloom map (updated in real time during the season) and the festival's social media are the practical tools for finding the day's best location. On festival weekends, local volunteers and signage direct visitors to the main sites.

## Getting there

**A hire car is strongly recommended.** The Eshkol bloom sites are in a rural area with no direct public transport. Beer Sheva — the nearest city, connected to Tel Aviv by rail in approximately 1 hour — is 25–40 km from the main bloom sites, but there is no onward bus service to the fields.

On **festival weekends**, the Darom Adom organisation typically runs shuttle buses from designated meeting points. Current shuttle details are announced on the festival website before each peak weekend.

**Without a car**: organised day tours from Tel Aviv and Jerusalem that cover the bloom sites are available via GetYourGuide and similar platforms during bloom season. These include transport and a guide who can navigate to the best current-day bloom location.

**Driving from Beer Sheva**: take Route 40 south, then Route 2357 west toward the Eshkol region. Journey time from Beer Sheva is approximately 30–40 minutes depending on the specific site.

## Accommodation

**Beer Sheva** is the practical base for Darom Adom — it has the widest range of accommodation closest to the bloom areas, rail connection to Tel Aviv, and amenities for multi-night stays.

**Netivot** (the nearest town to several bloom sites; approximately 15 km from Shokeda Forest) has limited but growing accommodation options and positions you closest to the festival areas.

**Mitzpe Ramon** (approximately 90 minutes from the Eshkol bloom sites) offers a different desert experience — the Ramon Crater and its boutique hotels are a viable base for combining Darom Adom with a longer Negev trip.

Book accommodation well ahead for the peak bloom weekend — the festival draws large Israeli domestic crowds, and hotels within reasonable driving distance fill quickly once bloom dates are announced.

---

## Security situation in the Eshkol region

The Eshkol region is in southern Israel, near the Gaza border. The area was significantly affected by the events of October 7, 2023, and many communities in the region experienced severe hardship.

By 2026, the Darom Adom festival and wildflower tourism have resumed. The Eshkol Regional Council and the festival organisation are actively welcoming visitors, and the resumption of the festival is part of the region's recovery and rebuilding effort.

**Before travelling to the Eshkol region**, check the current travel advisory from the Israeli government at [gov.il](https://www.gov.il) and your home country's foreign ministry advisory for Israel. Follow any local authority guidance during your visit. The festival organisers and Eshkol Regional Council website are the best source of current-year access and on-the-ground safety information.

---

## One rule: don't pick the flowers

Picking a kalanit (wild anemone) in Israel carries a statutory fine of **₪730**. More importantly: the existing bloom is the result of decades of INPA conservation work following a period when the flowers were nearly picked to local extinction by well-meaning visitors. Photographing from the paths, lying down among the flowers for a shot, and appreciating the bloom in situ is the right approach — and what protects the spectacle for future years.
