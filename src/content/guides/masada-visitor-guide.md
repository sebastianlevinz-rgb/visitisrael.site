Masada is a flat-topped fortress rising about 400 metres above the western shore of the Dead Sea, and it is Israel's most visited national park. Herod the Great built a palace complex on the summit in the first century BCE; a century later it became the last stronghold of Jewish rebels against Rome, a siege that ended in 73 CE and that is still told on the summit today. Most visitors arrive on an organised day trip that combines the fortress with the Ein Gedi oasis and a float in the [Dead Sea](/dead-sea). This guide covers both ways of doing it: how to visit Masada independently, and how the tours from [Jerusalem](/jerusalem) and [Tel Aviv](/tel-aviv) compare.

## The three ways up

All three routes reach the same plateau. The ruins and the views are identical; only the journey changes.

| Route | Effort | When | Who it suits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Snake Path (daytime)** | Moderate, 45–75 min | Winter and spring, or before 9 am in summer | Fit walkers who want to earn the summit |
| **Cable car** | None, about 3 min | Any time from around 8 am | Families, limited mobility, summer heat |
| **Snake Path at sunrise** | Moderate, in the dark | Gate opens around 4 am year-round | The memorable version; photographers |

### Snake Path

The Snake Path climbs the eastern face of the rock in a long series of switchbacks with stone steps on the steeper sections and handrails near the top. It gains roughly 350 metres over 1.5 km. The surface is rocky and uneven throughout, so shoes with grip matter more than fitness.

In July and August the path closes for several hours around midday because the exposed rock face regularly passes 40°C. If you want to hike in summer, be on the path before 8 am or ride the cable car up and walk down after the closure lifts; the descent takes 30 to 40 minutes and is far less punishing.

### Cable car

The cable car leaves from the eastern visitor centre and reaches the summit in about three minutes. It usually starts running around 8 am and stops roughly an hour before the park closes. Between May and September the queue builds after 9 am, so arriving at opening time saves real waiting. One-way tickets are sold, which makes the hike-up, ride-down combination easy.

### The sunrise hike

The sunrise version is the one people talk about. You start the Snake Path in darkness by head torch, reach the summit as the sky lightens and watch the sun clear the mountains of Jordan while the Dead Sea turns from grey to copper below you. In good conditions the light lasts 30 to 40 minutes after sunrise and the plateau is nearly empty.

Doing it independently: drive to the eastern car park (Masada Junction, Route 90), arrive by 4 am when the gate opens, and buy your park ticket online beforehand because nothing is staffed at that hour. Bring a head torch, two litres of water, a warm layer for the pre-dawn cold and a charged phone.

## What to see on the summit

Allow at least two hours. The fortress is much bigger than it looks from below.

- **Northern Palace.** Herod's private residence steps down the northern cliff tip in three terraces, with mosaic floors, painted plaster and the best views over the Dead Sea from the lower terrace.
- **Western Palace.** The largest structure on the plateau and the administrative centre, with some of the finest mosaic floors in the country and bathhouses with intact underfloor heating.
- **The synagogue.** One of the oldest identifiable synagogue buildings anywhere, in use during the revolt of 66–73 CE. Fragments of biblical scrolls were found here.
- **Byzantine church.** A 6th-century chapel with a well-preserved mosaic floor, built by monks who lived on the summit long after the siege.
- **Roman siege works.** From the western edge you look straight down on the siege ramp and the circumvallation wall, still remarkably complete after two thousand years.
- **Cisterns.** Flood channels filled enormous rock-cut cisterns during the rare winter rains, which is how a desert fortress held out for months.

## Masada tours compared

Because Masada, Ein Gedi and Ein Bokek sit within 30 km of each other on Route 90, almost every tour bundles them into one day. The real choice is between the sunrise format and the daytime format, and between a group coach and a private guide.

| Format | Pickup | Duration | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sunrise + Snake Path + Ein Gedi + Dead Sea** | 3–4 am | 12–14 hrs | First visit; the iconic light; summer |
| **Daytime cable car + Dead Sea** | 7–8 am | 9–11 hrs | Families; winter; anyone avoiding the climb |
| **Masada + Dead Sea self-drive** | Flexible | 7–10 hrs | Car renters who want to linger |
| **Private guide and driver** | Flexible | 8–12 hrs | Families; custom stops such as Qumran |

**Sunrise tours** are the bestseller for good reason. A coach collects you between 3 and 4 am from Jerusalem or Tel Aviv, you climb by torchlight, explore the ruins in the first light, ride the cable car down, stop at the Ein Gedi nature reserve for a short walk to the lower waterfall, and finish with an hour or so floating at Ein Bokek. In summer this is the sensible choice because you are off the plateau before the heat. The cost is the alarm clock and a very long day.

**Daytime tours** leave at a civilised hour, ride the cable car up and walk the ruins in the morning, then continue to the Dead Sea for the afternoon. Between November and March, when the sun rises later and the midday heat is mild, this format loses very little to the sunrise version and gains several hours of sleep. For families with children under ten it is clearly the better choice.

**From Jerusalem or from Tel Aviv?** Both cities have daily departures. Jerusalem is about 90 minutes from Masada; Tel Aviv adds roughly an hour each way, and sunrise pickups there can start before 3 am. If you have a choice of base, [Jerusalem](/best-hotels-jerusalem) makes the day shorter.

**What tours cost.** Guided group days generally run between US$85 and US$135 per person as of 2026, with sunrise formats and Tel Aviv departures at the upper end. Check what is included: national park entry and the cable car are sometimes paid on the spot, and lunch and beach lockers are almost always extra. Private guide-and-driver days are priced per vehicle rather than per person, so they become competitive for four or more people. For a fuller comparison of Dead Sea-only formats, see [Dead Sea tours compared](/dead-sea-tours-compared).

## Ein Gedi and the Dead Sea float

**Ein Gedi Nature Reserve**, 20 minutes north of Masada, is a spring-fed canyon of pools and waterfalls where ibex graze along the paths. Most tours include a 30 to 45 minute walk to the lower David waterfall; some skip it, so read the itinerary if it matters to you. Note that the Ein Gedi public beach on the Dead Sea has been closed for years because of sinkholes, so the float itself happens at Ein Bokek, 20 minutes south of Masada.

At **Ein Bokek** you float in water roughly ten times saltier than the ocean. Twenty minutes is plenty. Do not shave that morning, keep the water away from your eyes, bring water shoes for the crystallised shore and rinse in the freshwater showers afterwards. For beach passes, resort hotels and staying overnight, see the [Dead Sea hotels guide](/dead-sea-hotels-guide).

## Sound and Light Show

Masada runs an open-air sound and light show on the western slope on selected evenings from roughly March to October, usually Tuesdays and Thursdays. It retells the siege with lighting and narration and requires no climbing. It only makes sense if you are sleeping at Ein Bokek or Arad, since it ends too late for a return to Jerusalem or Tel Aviv. Dates and ticket prices change each season; check the park's official schedule before planning around it.

## Practical details

**Getting there.** Masada is on Route 90, about 90 minutes from Jerusalem via Route 1 and around two hours and a quarter from Tel Aviv. Public buses reach Ein Bokek and the Masada junction but do not connect with sunrise times. If you are driving, our [car rental guide](/car-rental-israel) covers the practicalities.

**Opening hours and tickets.** The Snake Path gate opens around 4 am; the visitor centre and cable car around 8 am. The park closes at about 5 pm in summer and 4 pm in winter. Entry fees change annually, so check parks.org.il. The Israel National Parks Pass covers Masada entry (but not the cable car) and pays for itself if you also visit Ein Gedi, Qumran or Caesarea.

**When to go.** October to April is the comfortable window; in December and January the plateau can be chilly at dawn but the midday visit is ideal. June to August is workable only at sunrise or by cable car with a short summit visit. See [best time to visit Israel](/best-time-to-visit-israel) for the seasonal picture.

**What to bring.** Two to three litres of water per person, hat and sunscreen, walking shoes, a head torch for the dark climb, swimsuit and towel for the Dead Sea, and a small bag for wet kit. There is a kiosk at the summit but no reliable water supply on the path.
