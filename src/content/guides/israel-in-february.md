February is Israel at its most surprising. The Negev hillsides and coastal plain erupt in carpets of red anemones — one of the Mediterranean world's most dramatic wildflower blooms, almost entirely unknown outside Israel. Mount Hermon is skiing. The Dead Sea is at its most comfortable. Eilat sits at 26°C while Europe is at its coldest. And Jerusalem and Tel Aviv are at their quietest and most affordable, the summer frenzy months away.

## February in two modes

February divides naturally across purpose and geography:

**Nature and outdoors February (Negev, Galilee, Golan):** Kalaniot wildflower season is the headline, but February also brings almond blossom along the Galilee hillsides, excellent Negev hiking conditions, and the best chance of skiable snow at Mount Hermon. The entire country is green from winter rains — a side of Israel that almost no summer visitor ever sees.

**Winter-sun February (Eilat, Dead Sea):** The Red Sea coast and the Dead Sea offer the same warm-weather escape as December, but with the added benefit of the wildflower bloom happening simultaneously in the desert interior. European visitors combine an Eilat beach base with a Negev day trip for the full February picture.

## Kalaniot anemone wildflower season

February is the month that turns the Negev red.

The kalanit (red anemone) is Israel's national flower — and in a good rainfall year, the last two weeks of February see the Negev desert and coastal plain transform into one of the most striking wildflower landscapes in the Mediterranean. Hillsides, fields, and roadsides fill with carpets of red, pink, and white anemones. At peak bloom, certain areas — particularly around Kibbutz Ruhama in the Eshkol region, and the Halutza Sands west of Beersheba — look almost impossibly vivid.

### Darom Adom festival

Darom Adom ('Red South') is the annual festival organised around the peak bloom by the Eshkol Regional Council. It typically takes place over one or two weekends in late February or early March, once bloom conditions are confirmed. The festival includes:

- Designated bloom hiking trails with depth markers showing carpet density
- Bedouin hospitality tents with traditional tea and food
- Guided nature walks and photography walks
- Family activities and live music on peak festival weekends

The festival is free at most sites. Because bloom timing depends on winter rainfall, the exact festival dates are only announced 2–4 weeks in advance. **Check [daromadom.co.il](https://www.daromadom.co.il) for current-year dates, trail maps, and real-time bloom reports before planning.**

Arrive early on peak festival weekends (before 9am) — the bloom fields draw large crowds from Tel Aviv and Beer Sheva, and parking fills quickly.

### Bloom timing and honesty

The kalaniot bloom peaks in mid-to-late February in a high-rainfall year; a dry winter can push it to early March or produce a thin bloom. **Do not book a wildflower-specific trip without checking bloom status the week before travel.** Darom Adom's website updates bloom intensity regularly from January onwards.

## Mount Hermon skiing

February is Israel's best month for snow.

Mount Hermon in the Golan Heights is Israel's only ski resort — the northernmost point of the country, at 2,814m at its highest accessible peak. The ski area operates between approximately 1,600m and 2,200m, with lifts, rental equipment, and a ski school available on site.

### What to expect

Mount Hermon skiing is a genuine Israeli experience rather than an Alpine comparison. The runs are intermediate in character; the mountain is scenic (views over the Golan, Galilee, and into Lebanon and Syria on clear days); and the crowd skews toward Israeli families and first-timers rather than expert skiers.

**What February offers:** Snow is more reliable in February than in any other month — but Israeli skiing should always be approached honestly. Not every February has skiable conditions. Check current snow reports at [skihermon.co.il](https://www.skihermon.co.il) (the official resort site) before booking; snow conditions are published regularly throughout winter.

**Getting there:** The mountain is about 2.5 hours from Tel Aviv by car; also reachable from Tiberias (1 hour). Guided day trips handle transport, which is often the most practical option if you don't want to navigate mountain roads in potentially icy conditions.

For the full guide — run grades, rental costs, ski school, and what to do on no-snow days — see the [Mount Hermon ski guide](/mount-hermon-skiing).

## Almond blossom in the Galilee

February is almond season — the first visible sign that winter is ending.

Almond trees bloom before any leaves appear, covering the hillsides of the Galilee and the Judean foothills in white and pale pink blossom as early as late January, with the main display typically in the first two weeks of February. The bloom is especially visible along the roads between Nazareth and Karmiel, around the Beit Keshet Forest, and in the valleys approaching Mount Carmel.

The almond blossom is not a festival or event — it is simply there, along roadsides and on hillsides, visible from the car window and spectacular when hiking. The Israel Nature and Parks Authority occasionally organises guided blossom walks; check their site for current-season programmes.

## The Dead Sea in February

February is one of the best months to visit the Dead Sea.

The water temperature sits at 20–23°C — warm enough to float comfortably without the dangerous heat of summer (43°C extremes in July make any extended outdoor activity before 9am or after 4pm genuinely hazardous). The air is 20–24°C in the day, cooling to 12–15°C at night. The resort strip at Ein Bokek is quieter than during Israeli school holiday peaks and significantly less crowded than July.

### Why February works so well

European visitors seeking a warm-weather escape in February find the Dead Sea genuinely compelling: the therapeutic float, the mineral-rich mud treatments at resort spas, and the landscape itself (the Jordan mountains across the water, the desert light in winter quality) combine into an experience that is meaningfully different from a summer visit.

**The caveat:** Israeli school holidays in February (the 'winter break', dates vary annually by the Hebrew calendar) see Dead Sea resort hotels fill and prices rise. Check Israeli school holiday dates for the year you're travelling and book ahead if your stay coincides with the break.

For beach choices, floating safety tips, and the full Ein Bokek accommodation map, see the [Dead Sea guide](/dead-sea-guide).

## Eilat and the Red Sea in February

Eilat in February delivers what European visitors come for: reliable sun at 24–26°C while northern Europe is cold and grey.

The Red Sea water temperature is 22–24°C — excellent for snorkelling and diving, and comfortable for swimming. The Coral Beach Nature Reserve is active. The surrounding air temperature is 26–28°C in the day, dropping to 14–16°C at night — warm enough for bare arms at midday but requiring a layer after dark.

### Diving and snorkelling

The Coral Beach Nature Reserve — Eilat's protected reef ecosystem — offers good winter visibility. A 3mm wetsuit is comfortable for longer dives in February water. The reef itself is accessible by snorkel directly from the shore of the nature reserve.

For the full Eilat activity map, beach guide, and accommodation tiers, see the [Eilat travel guide](/eilat-travel-guide).

## Negev and Ramon Crater hiking

February is the best hiking month in the Negev.

The desert is cool (16–20°C daytime, 8–12°C at night), clear, and still green from winter rains. The light is dramatic — long morning shadows across the crater walls, golden afternoon colour on the Negev stone. The Makhtesh Ramon (Ramon Crater) is at its most accessible in winter: no heat danger, comfortable temperatures for multi-hour walks, and the unusual February sight of desert wildflowers in the crater's more sheltered corners.

### Recommended hiking in February

**Makhtesh Ramon rim trail:** The classic 8km circuit around the crater's accessible edge. Start from the Mitzpe Ramon visitor centre. Allow 3–4 hours. No technical difficulty; some exposed rocky sections.

**The crater floor:** A 4WD vehicle or guided jeep tour covers the crater floor's geological highlights — the multicoloured sandstone formations, the prism-shaped ammonite fossils, and the dramatic scale of the crater walls. The Prism/Ammonite area is reachable on foot from designated parking areas.

**Sde Boker area:** The Ben-Gurion graves overlook in the Negev highlands, with walks down into the Zin Canyon, is especially beautiful in February when the canyon walls carry residual winter colour.

For crater hiking logistics and accommodation in Mitzpe Ramon, see the [Mitzpe Ramon guide](/mitzpe-ramon-guide).

## Tu BiShvat (New Year of Trees)

Tu BiShvat typically falls in late January or early February (in 2027: February 10; dates shift annually per the Hebrew calendar — always verify at [hebcal.com](https://www.hebcal.com)).

For tourists, Tu BiShvat is a non-disruptive minor holiday — shops, restaurants, transport, and all tourist sites operate normally. The visible elements: public parks and city councils organise tree-planting events; schools may hold planting activities; markets sell dried fruit and nuts that reference the holiday's seven sacred species. Some religious households hold a Tu BiShvat seder with symbolic fruit and wine courses. A pleasant encounter if you come across a community planting event, but no advance planning is needed.

## Jerusalem in February

Jerusalem in February is cold, quiet, and atmospheric.

**Temperatures:** 10–14°C daytime, 4–8°C evenings. A warm jacket is essential. Rain is frequent (January and February are Jerusalem's rainiest months). Snow on the Old City is rare but possible — and when it falls, it is extraordinary.

**Why visit in February:** Jerusalem in February has the lowest hotel rates of any month outside Yom Kippur week. The Old City is genuinely uncrowded compared to any school-holiday period. The winter light is a photographer's month — low angle, golden late afternoons, dramatic clouds over the Dome of the Rock. The major sites (Western Wall, Church of the Holy Sepulchre, Temple Mount) are accessible without the summer queue and heat.

**The snow possibility:** Jerusalem receives snow roughly once every few years, usually in January or February. When it falls, the Old City under snow is one of the most memorable images in Israel — the stone streets and ancient walls transformed. If snow is forecast during your Jerusalem trip, stay an extra day.

## Tel Aviv in February

Tel Aviv in February is genuinely the best-value month to visit.

**Temperatures:** 15–19°C daytime, 10–13°C evenings. Cool but not cold; a light jacket suffices. Rain is possible but Tel Aviv's winter is drier than Jerusalem's.

**Prices:** Hotel rates in February are among the lowest of the year — significantly below the April–October tourist season, and competitive with January. The beach is too cool for most swimming, but the promenade (Tayelet) is walkable and pleasant.

**Cultural season:** Tel Aviv's museums, galleries, and theatres run their strongest programming from October through March. The Tel Aviv Museum of Art, the ANU Museum of the Jewish People (previously Museum of the Diaspora), and the Eretz Israel Museum all have full winter programmes. The Cameri Theatre and Habima National Theatre run major February productions.

**Practicality as a base:** Tel Aviv in February is an excellent base for day trips — to the Negev wildflowers (2 hours), to Jerusalem (under 1 hour), to the Galilee (under 2 hours). The combination of affordable Tel Aviv hotel rates with wildflower-season day trips is one of the best-value Israel itineraries of the year.

**Almond blossom** (late January to mid-February): Galilee hillsides and Judean foothills. No events; simply visible along roads and hiking trails.

## Practical February planning

### Weather by region

| Region | Daytime | Evening | Conditions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eilat / Red Sea | 24–26°C | 14–16°C | Sunny, very low rain |
| Dead Sea | 20–24°C | 12–15°C | Mostly sunny |
| Negev (Mitzpe Ramon) | 16–20°C | 6–10°C | Cool, clear, ideal hiking |
| Tel Aviv / coast | 15–19°C | 10–13°C | Mild, intermittent rain |
| Jerusalem | 10–14°C | 4–8°C | Cold, frequent rain, possible snow |
| Galilee / Golan | 8–14°C | 3–7°C | Cold, rainy, green |
| Mount Hermon | –2–4°C | –10–2°C | Alpine; snow possible |

### What to pack

**Eilat / Dead Sea:** Light clothing, swimwear, SPF 50+. An evening layer for cooler nights.

**Negev hiking:** Layers. The Negev in February can be warm at midday (18–20°C) and surprisingly cold by late afternoon as desert temperatures drop rapidly after sunset. Start hikes at dawn if possible — the light is best and temperatures are manageable.

**Jerusalem and Tel Aviv:** A genuine warm jacket. Rain jacket or umbrella for Jerusalem. Waterproof footwear for wet cobblestones.

**Religious sites (all regions):** Covered shoulders and knees regardless of outside temperature.

### Booking priorities

- **Dead Sea over Israeli school break (dates vary annually):** 4–6 weeks ahead minimum.
- **Eilat in February generally:** 2–3 weeks ahead is usually sufficient outside school breaks.
- **Jerusalem in February (outside school breaks):** Good availability; 1–2 weeks is often adequate.
- **Tel Aviv in February:** Easiest availability of any tourist month — last-minute bookings often possible.
- **Darom Adom festival weekends:** Negev guesthouses and glamping sites near the bloom area sell out quickly once festival dates are announced. Book within 48 hours of announcement.
