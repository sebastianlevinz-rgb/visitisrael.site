The Tamar Festival is Israel's largest annual Sukkot music event — a week of orchestral, chamber, jazz, and world-music concerts performed on an open-air stage by the Dead Sea, with the Judean Desert cliffs rising behind the audience and the lights of Jordan reflected across the water at night. Since its founding in 1999, the festival has drawn Israel's leading orchestras and international soloists to one of the world's most dramatic natural concert settings.

This guide covers the 2026 festival format, Dead Sea hotel strategy for Sukkot week, how to combine the festival with a Masada sunrise, and practical logistics from Jerusalem and Tel Aviv.

---

## What is the Tamar Festival?

The Tamar Festival (Hebrew: פסטיבל תמר) was established in 1999 in the Tamar Region of the southern Dead Sea area and has grown into Israel's signature Sukkot cultural event. The festival runs during the Chol HaMoed intermediate days of Sukkot — typically 4–6 evenings spread across the holiday week — with concerts ranging from afternoon chamber recitals to late-night orchestral programmes.

The lineup each year spans classical repertoire (symphonies, concertos, chamber music), jazz, and world music, featuring a mix of Israeli ensembles — including regular appearances by the Israel Philharmonic Orchestra and the Israel Chamber Orchestra — alongside international soloists and ensembles. Attendance across the festival week runs to approximately 25,000 visitors.

The setting is fundamental to the festival's identity: an open-air amphitheatre stage at the Dead Sea shore, with the Judean Desert hills behind the stage and the dark salt-water expanse of the Dead Sea — the lowest point on Earth — forming the natural backdrop. Late September and early October evenings at the Dead Sea are warm (25–30°C at dusk), calm, and reliably clear — conditions that make outdoor classical music here genuinely special.

## 2026 dates and Sukkot context

**Sukkot 2026** begins the evening of Thursday **24 September** (first night). The Chol HaMoed intermediate days run from Saturday 26 September through Thursday 1 October, with Hoshana Raba on Thursday 1 October and Shemini Atzeret/Simchat Torah closing the festival on Friday 2 October (evening) through Saturday 3 October.

The Tamar Festival occupies the Chol HaMoed days — expect the main concert programme to run across Saturday 26 September through Wednesday or Thursday (1 October). **Exact 2026 dates, programme, and ticket details are published on the official Tamar Festival website** (tamarmusicfest.com) approximately 6–8 weeks before the festival. Simchat Torah closures can affect the final festival day, so check the calendar before confirming your travel.

The Sukkot period is the Dead Sea's peak demand week of the year: Israeli families flock to the resort hotels, and occupancy in Ein Bokek reaches 95–100% by mid-July for the holiday week. Book accommodation and transport well in advance.

## Getting there

**From Jerusalem** (approximately 70 km south via Route 90; 1 hour 15 minutes by car):

Route 90 hugs the western shore of the Dead Sea south from Jericho — one of the most atmospheric drives in Israel, descending past sea-level markers to −430 m at the Dead Sea shore. Drive south past Qumran, Ein Gedi, and Kibbutz Ein Gedi to reach the festival area near Ein Gedi/the Tamar region.

Festival shuttle buses from Jerusalem have been offered in previous editions for ticketholders — check tamarmusicfest.com for the 2026 shuttle programme and pick-up locations. Without a shuttle, a taxi from Jerusalem to the Dead Sea festival site runs approximately ₪300–₪450 one way; agree the price before departing.

**From Tel Aviv** (approximately 100 km; 1 hour 30 minutes by car):

Take Route 1 east toward Jerusalem, then Route 90 south along the Dead Sea shore. Driving directly is more convenient than routing through Jerusalem — the Route 1–90 junction near Jericho/Almog is well signposted.

**From the Ein Bokek resort strip**:

If you are staying at the main Dead Sea resort hotels (Isrotel, Herods, Leonardo), the festival site is approximately 20 minutes north along Route 90. A hire car is the simplest option for the evening commute. Taxis are available from the Ein Bokek taxi rank but demand is high on festival evenings — arrange your return in advance.

**Parking** at the festival site fills quickly. Festival-goers who arrive later than 60–90 minutes before the first concert often park along Route 90 and walk. Check the official site for 2026 parking arrangements.

## Dead Sea hotel strategy for Sukkot

The Ein Bokek hotel strip is the most practical base for Tamar Festival visitors — it places you 20 minutes from the festival site with the full range of Dead Sea beach and spa activities on your doorstep.

**Main resort options** (Ein Bokek):
- **Isrotel Dead Sea** — the largest Dead Sea resort; multiple pools, private beach, full spa
- **Herods Dead Sea** — upscale; all-inclusive option available; good pool zone
- **Leonardo Club Dead Sea** — mid-market; all-inclusive format; popular with Israeli families
- **Leonardo Inn Dead Sea** — more affordable within the cluster; standard rooms, shared pool access

**Alternative base — Kibbutz Ein Gedi** (approximately 20 minutes north of Ein Bokek): the Kibbutz Ein Gedi hotel and guesthouses offer a quieter, more nature-focused alternative with access to the Ein Gedi oasis and botanical garden. This base is closer to the festival site (15 minutes) but further from the main spa and beach infrastructure of Ein Bokek.

**Jerusalem as base**: viable for those who cannot secure Dead Sea accommodation. The ~1h15m drive from Jerusalem to the festival site is manageable, but late-night return after a long concert programme is tiring — particularly on Shabbat-transition evenings when fuel stations along Route 90 may have reduced hours.

**Booking window**: Sukkot Dead Sea hotels sell out 3–4 months in advance at reasonable rates. By July, only higher-priced rooms and last-minute cancellations remain. If you are reading this in July or August 2026, check immediately — availability narrows quickly.

## Combine the festival with Masada

The classic Tamar Festival day:

**07:30** — Masada cable car ascent at sunrise. Masada National Park is 20 km south of the festival site on Route 90. The cable car opens at 08:00; late September sunrise is around 06:20–06:30 — arriving at the lower cable car station by 06:00 for the queue on a Sukkot Chol HaMoed morning is advisable (the site is popular). The summit circuit covers Herod's northern palace, the Roman siege ramp view, the casemate wall, the synagogue, and the Byzantine church — allow 2–3 hours.

**11:00** — Descend and drive to Ein Bokek for the Dead Sea float. The Ein Bokek public beach allows free floating access. The buoyancy of the Dead Sea water (33–34% salinity) is a genuinely distinctive experience — unlike swimming in any other body of water on Earth. Limit time in the water to 15–20 minutes and rinse the salt thoroughly afterward. Shade becomes important from midday onward.

**13:00–18:00** — Lunch and rest at your hotel or Ein Bokek promenade restaurants. The long afternoon before the evening concert is a good time for the spa, pool, or a quieter walk to the shoreline.

**19:00–20:00** — Drive to the Tamar Festival venue (20–30 minutes from Ein Bokek) and take your seats for the evening programme.

This sequence makes use of the Dead Sea's geography — Masada and the festival site are both on the same Route 90 corridor south of Jerusalem — and avoids the midday desert heat for strenuous outdoor activity.

## Ein Gedi: the day-trip add-on

Ein Gedi Nature Reserve and National Park is approximately 10 km north of the festival area along Route 90 — a worthwhile half-day add-on for hikers.

The reserve protects two river canyons (Nahal David and Nahal Arugot) fed by year-round freshwater springs descending from the Judean Hills to the Dead Sea shore. The Nahal David trail (2.5 km return; moderate; no technical equipment needed) passes Shulamit Spring and David's Waterfall — a 20-metre cascade with a natural pool at the base, surrounded by date palms and ibex that are accustomed to visitors. The canyon temperature is noticeably cooler than the Dead Sea shore below.

Enter via the INPA ticket gate (Israel National Parks Pass valid). The reserve is best visited in the morning before midday heat — plan entry by 08:00–09:00 and exit by 11:30.
