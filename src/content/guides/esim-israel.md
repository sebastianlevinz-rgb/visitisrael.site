For most visitors the answer is an international eSIM bought before you fly: **Airalo or Holafly** if you want unlimited data (about US$26–28 for a week at prices read on 23 September 2026), or a cheaper capped plan from **Nomad, aloSIM or Ubigi** (around US$12–19 for 10 GB) if you mostly use maps and WhatsApp. A physical SIM at Ben Gurion only makes sense if you need an Israeli phone number.

Israel has four national mobile networks, 5G in every major city and 4G on practically every road you will drive. The connectivity problem is not coverage, it is price: roaming with a home carrier costs US$10–12 a day for many travellers, and the SIM shops at the airport charge more than the same operators do online. Below is what each option costs today, where each one stops working, and how to set yours up so it is live before the seatbelt sign goes off. Staying a month or more, or need an Israeli number? Skip to the local SIM section. Continuing to Petra? An Israel plan stops at the border; see the Jordan section.

## How an eSIM works, and how to know if your phone has one

An eSIM is a SIM card written into a chip already inside your phone. Instead of a plastic card, the provider sends a QR code or an activation code; you scan it, the phone downloads the profile, and the Israel plan sits alongside your home SIM as a second line. You choose which line handles data (the eSIM) and which handles calls and texts (your home number), and you turn the eSIM off the day you fly home.

To check compatibility:

- **iPhone**: every model from the XS, XR and 11 onward. Go to Settings › General › About and look for an "EID" line. iPhones sold in mainland China do not have eSIM, and some Hong Kong models are dual physical SIM only.
- **Android**: Google Pixel 3 and newer, Samsung Galaxy S20 and newer (and most later Galaxy Z, Note and A5x models), plus recent Motorola, OnePlus, Xiaomi and Oppo flagships. Dial `*#06#` and look for an EID, or check Settings › Network › SIMs for an "Add eSIM" option. Carrier-locked phones cannot add an eSIM until they are unlocked, so ask your carrier before you travel.
- **Not compatible**: phones without an EID and most Chinese-market handsets. For those, the choice is a physical SIM at the airport or roaming.

Every provider below has a compatibility checker on its site. Use it, because refunds for "my phone did not support it" are at the provider's discretion.

## International eSIM vs local SIM vs roaming

### International eSIM

**Buy it if:** your phone is compatible, you are staying up to about three weeks and you do not need a local number. That is most people reading this. You install it at home on Wi-Fi, land, turn on the line and you are online before passport control; prices are in your currency, support is in your language, and there is no queue or passport registration. Airalo and Holafly both let you top up from the app. **Where it loses:** price per gigabyte over long stays, and anything that needs an Israeli number.

### Local Israeli SIM or eSIM

Israel has four network operators, **Partner, Cellcom, Pelephone and HOT Mobile**, plus virtual operators such as **019 Mobile** (on the Partner network) and **Golan Telecom**. All sell tourist packages with a local number, unlimited calls inside Israel and large data allowances, typically for 10 or 30 days.

The operators' own sites publish prices mostly in Hebrew and were not readable for us on 23 September 2026, so the figures here come from two dated sources. Traveltomtom's September 2026 survey lists tourist packages roughly between ₪50 and ₪130 for 30 days with anywhere from 35 GB to several hundred GB plus unlimited local calls, depending on operator and shop. 019 Mobile's own English tourist page, read the same day, lists US$29 for 10 GB over 10 days, US$39 for 30 GB over 30 days and US$49 for 100 GB over 30 days, each with an Israeli number, unlimited local calls and texts, and free landline calls to 40 countries, when ordered online for collection at the airport.

**Buy it if:** you are staying a month or more, you need an Israeli number (some apartment rentals, deliveries and bank SMS codes still ask for one), or your phone has no eSIM. Registration with a passport is required, and the operators' eSIM offers are aimed at residents with Hebrew-language sign-up, which is why nearly every tourist who wants an eSIM ends up with an international one.

### Buying a SIM at Ben Gurion Airport

Two shops in the Terminal 3 arrivals hall sell tourist SIMs: the **019 Mobile** store, which its site says is open 24 hours a day six days a week (closed for Shabbat), and the **Steimatzky** bookshop, which sells Partner packages. Other operators' counters come and go.

The catch is price. Traveltomtom's September 2026 visit found 019 selling 10 GB for 8 days at US$45 and unlimited data for 8 days at US$54 over the counter, and Partner via Steimatzky at ₪230 for 150 GB over 10 days; 019's own site says its online pre-order price is about 30 % below the walk-in price. None of the airport shops sold eSIMs at the time of writing. If you want a local SIM, order it online and collect it in the arrivals hall, or buy it in a city phone shop the next day.

### Roaming with your home carrier

**United States**: Verizon's TravelPass costs US$12 per day per line in Israel (read on verizon.com, 23 September 2026), with a US$100 monthly international plan for longer trips. AT&T's International Day Pass is priced in the same range; T-Mobile includes slow international data on many plans and sells a US$5 day pass for full speed. A week of TravelPass costs more than three weeks of an unlimited eSIM.

**European Union and UK**: Israel is not part of EU "roam like at home". Operators either bill per megabyte or sell world passes: Deutsche Telekom's Travel Mobil options for its Zone 3, which includes Israel, start at €49.95 a month according to its roaming page; Orange France sells 7- or 31-day Pass Voyage bundles for its Asia zone, which includes Israel. Check your operator's exact zone before you assume anything.

**Argentina and Latin America**: Personal, Movistar and Claro sell "resto del mundo" packs of a couple of gigabytes valid for 30 days at peso prices that change month to month; per gigabyte they cost several times what any eSIM in the table charges. Claro includes free WhatsApp abroad, useful as a backup for messages, not for maps.

**Verdict:** roaming is the right choice only if your plan includes Israel at no or trivial cost. Otherwise it is the backup, not the plan.

## Coverage in Israel, region by region

Israel's four networks reach almost everyone who lives in the country, and tourists go where people live. Opensignal's most recent published Israel reports gave Partner the top score for 4G coverage experience and Pelephone the top score for 5G coverage, but all four are close.

- **[Tel Aviv](/tel-aviv), [Jerusalem](/jerusalem), [Haifa](/haifa) and the coast**: 5G in the centres, dense 4G everywhere else. Trains between the cities keep a signal the whole way.
- **[Galilee](/galilee) and Golan**: 4G in towns, on the main roads and around the Sea of Galilee. Signal drops in some valleys and on hiking trails; download offline maps for a day of walking.
- **[Dead Sea](/dead-sea)**: the Ein Bokek hotel zone, [Masada](/masada-visitor-guide) and Ein Gedi all have 4G. On the shore roads and viewpoints your phone may lock onto a Jordanian tower across the water. That is roaming in another country: set the network manually, or use airplane mode plus Wi-Fi when you stop, to avoid a surprise charge on your home SIM.
- **[Negev](/negev)**: Beersheba, Mitzpe Ramon, Sde Boker and Route 40 have coverage; inside the Ramon crater, on desert tracks and at trailheads it is patchy to none. Treat the desert as offline.
- **[Eilat](/eilat)**: full 4G and 5G in town and at the beaches. Near the Aqaba border your phone may again see Jordanian networks; same advice as at the Dead Sea.

One practical note: a travel eSIM appears to your phone as a foreign carrier, so "data roaming" must be on for the eSIM line even inside Israel. And on Shabbat the networks do not change, only the shops do: a SIM that fails on Friday evening waits until Saturday night for a counter.

## Bethlehem and the West Bank

The [Bethlehem day trip](/jerusalem-bethlehem-day-trip) is the most common reason a visitor crosses into the West Bank. Israeli networks cover the main roads and, because Bethlehem sits directly against Jerusalem, most of the city, so an Israel eSIM usually keeps working at the Church of the Nativity and Manger Square. It is not guaranteed, and your phone may jump between Israeli signal and none.

Deeper in the West Bank, in Ramallah, Jericho, Nablus and Hebron, the networks people use are the Palestinian operators **Jawwal** and **Ooredoo Palestine**, and Israel-only eSIMs do not include them. If your itinerary spends real time there, look for a plan that lists "Palestine" or "Palestinian Territories" among its countries (Airalo and Holafly both sell Palestine eSIMs), or buy a Palestinian SIM locally. For a guided Bethlehem day trip the fix is simpler: download the Bethlehem area in Google Maps offline before you leave Jerusalem.

## Petra and Jordan

Petra is in Jordan, another country with its own operators (Zain, Orange Jordan, Umniah). An Israel eSIM does not work there, and the Jordanian towers you meet at the Dead Sea or in Eilat are the ones that will bill your home SIM if roaming is on. For the [Petra trip from Israel](/petra-from-israel) you have three options:

1. **A separate Jordan eSIM.** Airalo's Jordan plan on Zain cost US$4.50 for 1 GB over 3 days on 23 September 2026, which covers a day trip comfortably.
2. **A regional Middle East eSIM** that lists both Israel and Jordan. Airalo's Middle East and North Africa plan (13 countries, unlimited data from US$24 for 3 days on the same date) is one; confirm both countries on the list before you buy, because coverage lists change.
3. **Go offline for the day.** Petra's visitor centre and most hotels in Wadi Musa have Wi-Fi, and organised tours do not need you online.

Install any Jordan eSIM at home too; there is no Wi-Fi at the border crossings.

## How to install and activate, step by step

### Before you fly

1. **Check compatibility and that your phone is unlocked**, then buy the plan online. Pick a validity that covers your whole stay from the day you land: most plans start counting only when the eSIM first connects to an Israeli network.
2. **Install the eSIM at home on Wi-Fi.** Scan the QR code from another screen or use the app's direct install (Airalo and Holafly both offer one-tap install on iPhone).
3. **Label it "Israel"** and, on iPhone, keep "Turn On This Line" enabled but leave **Mobile Data set to your home SIM** until you land. On Android, add the eSIM as a secondary SIM with data off.
4. **Set your home SIM's data roaming to off** so a background update does not trigger a roaming day when you land.
5. **Download offline maps** for Jerusalem, the Dead Sea and any desert or Galilee day, and save your hotel address and tour meeting points in a note.

### When you land

1. Turn on the Israel line and set it as the **default for Mobile Data**; leave calls and SMS on your home line.
2. Turn on **Data Roaming for the eSIM line**. This switch is the number-one reason "my eSIM does not work" tickets get opened.
3. If nothing happens after a minute, toggle airplane mode, or set the network manually to the operator named in your plan (Partner, Pelephone, HOT Mobile, Annatel).
4. Check the APN only if the provider tells you to; most profiles configure it automatically.
5. If it still fails, connect to the airport Wi-Fi and contact the provider's chat: both Airalo and Holafly run 24-hour support and can reissue a profile.

## WhatsApp, calls and a local number

A data-only eSIM does not give you a phone number, and that matters less than it sounds. **WhatsApp stays tied to your home number** and works over any data connection, so messages and WhatsApp calls carry on as before. Keep your home SIM installed, even with data off, so the app can keep verifying the number.

Israeli hotels, guides, tour operators and drivers live on WhatsApp and will message rather than call. Taxi apps (Gett and Uber, which in Israel dispatches licensed taxis) accept a foreign number. Emergency numbers (100 police, 101 ambulance, 102 fire) work from any phone, even one with no SIM.

You genuinely need an Israeli number only if a local service insists on an Israeli SMS code or you will be calling Israeli landlines a lot. Then a 019 Mobile or Partner tourist SIM is the right buy, and it can run alongside an eSIM.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Installing the eSIM at the airport.** Airport Wi-Fi is fine most days; the day it is not is the day you need it. Install at home.
- **Buying the plan for the wrong dates.** Validity starts at first connection; a 7-day plan for a 9-day trip means two days of scrambling. Round up.
- **Leaving home-SIM data roaming on.** One background update can cost a full roaming day before you have left the terminal.
- **Forgetting the data-roaming toggle on the eSIM line.** It must be on for a travel eSIM to connect.
- **Assuming the West Bank and Jordan are covered.** They are separate networks; see above.
- **Deleting the eSIM when you run out of data.** Top up instead; a fresh eSIM means a fresh install.
- **Buying at the airport shop without checking the online price.** The same 019 package is cheaper pre-ordered online, per 019's own site.
