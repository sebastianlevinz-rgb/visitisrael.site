The stretch of Mediterranean coast north of Tel Aviv holds three of Israel's most rewarding historic sites within an hour and a half of each other: **Caesarea**, Herod's Roman harbour city; **Haifa**, with the terraced Bahá'í Gardens stepping down Mount Carmel; and **Akko**, the most complete Crusader city in the region, still lived in above the vaulted halls. Guided tours from [Tel Aviv](/tel-aviv) string them into a single day, and it is one of the classic [day trips from Tel Aviv](/day-trips-from-tel-aviv). This guide explains what each stop offers, how the tour formats differ, what they cost, and how to do the same loop by car or train.

## The three stops

### Caesarea

Founded by Herod the Great around 25 BCE as the Roman provincial capital, Caesarea Maritima is the most compact major archaeological site in the country. The **Roman theatre**, restored and still used for summer concerts, is the usual first stop; the **hippodrome** where chariot races were held runs along the shore; and the outline of Herod's artificial harbour, built with hydraulic concrete poured underwater, can be traced from the Crusader walls that later enclosed the port. A small museum shows a replica of the Pilate inscription, the only stone that names Pontius Pilate as prefect of Judea. Ten minutes north, on a free beach outside the park, the **Roman aqueduct** marches along the sand for nearly two kilometres; it is the most photographed spot in Caesarea and is usually only reachable if you are driving yourself. Allow two hours for the national park.

### Haifa

Israel's third city climbs Mount Carmel above a working port, and its set piece is the **Bahá'í Gardens**: 19 terraces of lawns, cypresses and fountains running from the crest of the hill down to the German Colony, inscribed by UNESCO in 2008 and centred on the gold-domed Shrine of the Báb. Tours stop at the upper overlook on Yefe Nof Street for the view down to the sea, and often at the bottom of Ben-Gurion Avenue in the **German Colony**, where restored 19th-century Templer houses now hold cafés and restaurants. The inner terraces are open only on the free guided walk at noon, which most day tours cannot fit. With extra time, **Stella Maris**, the Carmelite monastery over the cave of Elijah on the headland, and the hummus institutions of **Wadi Nisnas** are the other Haifa stops. See the [Haifa region guide](/haifa) for the full picture.

### Akko

Akko (Acre) is the surprise of the day for most visitors. After Saladin took Jerusalem in 1187, the Crusaders made Akko their capital, and the **Knights' Halls** they built, a complex of vaulted Gothic spaces including a great hall and a refectory, were sealed by the Mamluks in 1291 and only fully excavated in recent decades. The same ticket includes the **Templar tunnel**, a 350-metre passage under the old city that comes out at the harbour. Above ground, the Ottoman city is intact and inhabited: the green-domed **Al-Jazzar Mosque**, the Khan al-Umdan caravanserai, a working souk and the sea walls looking back across the bay to Haifa. Lunch here is the best of the day, whether hummus in the market or grilled fish on the port. Allow two to two and a half hours.

## Tour formats compared

| Format | Duration | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Caesarea + Haifa + Akko from Tel Aviv** | 9–10 hrs | The standard loop; first visit to the north |
| **Caesarea + Haifa + Akko from Jerusalem** | 11–12 hrs | Jerusalem-based visitors without a car |
| **Akko + Rosh Hanikra (+ Haifa)** | 9–10 hrs | Second northern day; Crusader city and sea grottoes |
| **Self-drive** | Flexible | Adding Aqueduct Beach, a long lunch, Druze villages |
| **Train (Haifa + Akko)** | Flexible | Car-free travellers; the coastal line is pleasant |
| **Private guide and driver** | Flexible | Families; a Zichron Yaakov wine stop; slower pace |

**Standard guided day.** Pickup in central Tel Aviv around 8 am, Caesarea until late morning, Haifa for the gardens overlook and a short walk in the German Colony, Akko for the Knights' Halls, the tunnel and a late lunch, and back in Tel Aviv around 6 pm. It covers a lot of ground and the guide's context makes the layers at Caesarea and Akko legible, which they are not without explanation. The compromise is that each stop is a highlights visit; the aqueduct beach, Stella Maris and the Akko sea walls are usually left out.

**Rosh Hanikra variant.** Thirty kilometres north of Akko, on the border with Lebanon, a cable car drops from white chalk cliffs to grottoes hollowed out by the sea. Tours that include it pair it with Akko, sometimes with Haifa, and skip Caesarea. It is a good second northern day rather than a replacement for the first.

**From Jerusalem.** Adding an hour each way makes this a long day, but tours run, and for Jerusalem-based travellers without a car it is the only way to see the north coast in a day. Civitatis lists departures aimed at Spanish speakers, with other languages on some dates.

## What it costs

As of 2026, group day tours from Tel Aviv generally cost between US$95 and US$135 per person, with transport and a licensed guide included. Entry fees for Caesarea National Park and the Akko Knights' Halls are sometimes bundled and sometimes collected on the day; the Bahá'í Gardens public areas are free. Lunch is extra. If you are driving, the Israel National Parks Pass covers Caesarea (Akko's old city is run separately) and pays for itself if you also visit Masada or Ein Gedi.

## Doing it yourself

**By car.** Highway 2 north from Tel Aviv reaches the Caesarea exit in about 45 minutes. From Caesarea, continue north to Haifa (30 minutes) and on Highway 4 to Akko (25 minutes). Park outside Akko's old city; the lanes inside are pedestrian. Driving lets you add Aqueduct Beach, the Ralli Museum's free sculpture garden in Caesarea, or the Druze villages of Daliyat al-Karmel on the back of Mount Carmel. Total driving is roughly three hours for the loop. See our [car rental guide](/car-rental-israel).

**By train.** The coastal line from Tel Aviv Savidor stops at Caesarea-Pardes Hanna, Haifa Hof HaCarmel and Akko, roughly 40, 60 and 90 minutes respectively. Haifa's Carmelit funicular climbs from the lower city to the Bahá'í overlook. The train makes a Haifa and Akko day easy; Caesarea needs a taxi from the station, so most train-based visitors save it for another day. No trains from Friday afternoon to Saturday evening.

**Sequencing.** Go to Caesarea first, when the theatre is empty and the light on the harbour is low, and end in Akko, where the port is at its best in the late afternoon and the underground halls stay cool whatever the season.

## Practical details

**Heat and shade.** Caesarea is exposed; the Bahá'í overlook is a viewpoint rather than a walk; Akko's underground is cool. Spring and autumn are ideal, and summer is manageable with an early start. See [best time to visit Israel](/best-time-to-visit-israel).

**Dress.** Modest clothing for the Al-Jazzar Mosque and Stella Maris. Photography of the Bahá'í terraces is welcome; photography of pilgrims is not.

**Shabbat.** All three sites are open on Saturday, but public transport stops and the German Colony is quieter. The Arab-owned restaurants in Akko and Wadi Nisnas are open.

## Planning the rest of your trip

The north coast pairs naturally with the [Galilee](/galilee): from Akko, Nazareth is under an hour away, and many travellers spend a night in Haifa or Tiberias and do the [Nazareth and Sea of Galilee circuit](/nazareth-sea-of-galilee-day-trip) the next day. If you are based in [Jerusalem](/jerusalem), consider doing this day on the way north rather than as a return trip. Most [multi-day Israel packages](/israel-tour-packages) include Caesarea and Haifa on the drive between Tel Aviv and the Galilee.
