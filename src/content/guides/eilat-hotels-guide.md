Eilat sits at the southern tip of Israel on the Gulf of Aqaba, with Jordan visible across the water and Egypt a few kilometres down the coast. It is the country's beach resort, with several hundred hotels and a climate that stays warm from November to March when the rest of Israel is in jackets. The hotels sit in three distinct zones, and choosing between them matters more than choosing between individual properties. This guide maps the zones, explains what each price tier buys, and sets out when to book and when to stay away.

For what to do once you have arrived, from the reef to Timna Park to the trip across the border, see the [Eilat region guide](/eilat).

## The three hotel zones

### North Beach: the resort strip

North Beach is where most of Eilat's hotels stand: a two-kilometre arc from the marina and the lagoon in the north to the road junction near the Underwater Observatory in the south. The beach is public and free, the water is calm because the gulf has almost no wave action, and the promenade behind it holds the restaurants, bars, ice-cream counters and watersports rental. The large resort brands cluster at the lagoon end; mid-range hotels sit a street or two back.

**Best for:** first-time visitors, families, anyone who wants beach, restaurants and evening life on foot. The trade-off is scale: this is a busy strip, especially in Israeli school holidays.

### Coral Beach: the reef zone

Three to five kilometres south of the centre on the coast road, a smaller group of hotels sits beside the **Coral Beach Nature Reserve**, the only place in Israel where you can walk in from the shore and be over living reef within minutes. The dive centres are here too, and so is the Dolphin Reef. It is quieter and less built up than North Beach, with the desert mountains close behind.

**Best for:** snorkellers, divers, families who want the reef every morning, travellers who prefer calm to promenade. The trade-off is distance: the North Beach restaurants are a bus or taxi ride away.

### City centre: the budget zone

Behind the strip and around the central bus station, the city centre has the guesthouses, hostels, small hotels and holiday apartments. It is a 10 to 15 minute walk to North Beach and next to the malls and everyday shops, without sea views or promenade access.

**Best for:** budget travellers, anyone arriving by bus, and visitors who will spend their days on Petra, desert or diving trips rather than at the hotel.

## Hotels by price tier

Prices below are 2026 ranges for a double room and vary a great deal with the season; check live rates for your dates.

### Budget: roughly ₪350 to ₪600 per night

Genuine budget beds are almost entirely in the city centre. **Amdar Hostel** is the main backpacker address, with dorms and private rooms, clean and functional rather than atmospheric, and full of Israeli travellers in holiday periods. Small guesthouses such as the **Manta Ray Inn** offer private en-suite rooms a 12 to 15 minute walk from the beach. Along Sderot HaTmarim, the commercial road parallel to the strip, older mid-week hotels undercut the beachfront considerably. These are places to sleep and shower between activities, and they do that well.

### Mid-range: roughly ₪600 to ₪1,200 per night

This tier buys a pool, a decent breakfast and either a sea view or a short walk to the beach. On the North Beach promenade, the **Prima Music** has a rooftop pool and direct promenade access without flagship prices. South of the centre towards Coral Beach, the **Orchid** is a large garden resort with its own pool complex, styled after a Southeast Asian resort and popular with families who do not need the strip. The **U** boutique properties sit at the upper end of this tier with smaller room counts and more individual service. Breakfast is usually included at this level; confirm before booking.

### Luxury: roughly ₪1,200 to ₪3,000 and above

Eilat's top tier is dominated by Israeli resort brands with the infrastructure to be self-contained. The **Dan Eilat** on the lagoon is the benchmark North Beach resort: direct water access, several restaurants and a full spa, at the most animated corner of the strip. The **Isrotel Royal Beach** occupies the central beachfront with large pools, spa and evening entertainment; its sister property **Isrotel Yam Suf** at the southern end near Coral Beach is quieter and better placed for divers. The top of the range applies to sea-view suites and peak weeks; outside July, August and the Jewish holidays the same rooms cost a good deal less.

Eilat is in the middle of a large hotel-building cycle on the land of the old city-centre airport, with several upscale properties opening in phases through 2026 and 2027. Check live availability rather than assuming a newly announced hotel is taking bookings.

## Seasonal pricing

| Period | Weather | Demand and rates |
|---|---|---|
| **January to early March** | Warm days, cool sea, best visibility | Cheapest window; excellent for diving |
| **Late March to May** | Warm, building | Passover week is a peak; otherwise good value |
| **June to August** | Very hot, 40°C and above by day | Israeli summer holiday; highest rates of the year |
| **September** | Hot, sea at its warmest | Good shoulder month; fewer families |
| **October** | Warm | Sukkot week is a peak; the rest of the month is calm |
| **November to mid-December** | Warm days, mild evenings | Low rates; ideal for a winter-sun week |
| **Late December** | Mild | Christmas and New Year push prices up |

**Booking rules of thumb.** Three to four months ahead for July, August, Passover and Sukkot. Two to four weeks for most of November to March. Many hotels require two nights on weekends. See [best time to visit Israel](/best-time-to-visit-israel) for how Eilat's calendar fits the rest of the country.

## Choosing between zones

| Your priority | Where to stay |
|---|---|
| Beach, promenade and evenings on foot | North Beach |
| Reef snorkelling or diving daily | Coral Beach |
| Lowest cost | City centre |
| Pool infrastructure for children | North Beach lagoon end |
| Quiet, desert backdrop | Coral Beach |
| Petra and desert trips, hotel just for sleeping | City centre or a mid-range North Beach hotel |

## Getting there and around

Eilat's **Ramon Airport** is 20 minutes north of the city, with domestic flights from Tel Aviv and some seasonal European services; taxis and a bus connect it to the hotels. By road, Eilat is about four hours from Tel Aviv and three and a half from Jerusalem via the Dead Sea and the Arava, or via the [Negev](/negev) and Mitzpe Ramon, which is the more scenic route. Our [car rental guide](/car-rental-israel) covers the drive. Within the city, a local bus runs along the coast road to Coral Beach; North Beach and the centre are walkable.

## Combining Eilat with Petra

Half the reason international visitors come to Eilat is the [Petra trip](/petra-from-israel): the Jordanian border is ten minutes from the hotel strip and tours collect from every hotel. If Petra is the point, book two nights in Eilat around a one-day tour, or three nights around the two-day Petra and Wadi Rum version, and use the remaining time for the reef. That combination is also the usual final leg of our [10-day itinerary](/itineraries/10-days-in-israel).

## How Eilat compares

Eilat is a beach town with a resort strip; Ein Bokek on the Dead Sea is a resort strip with nothing behind it. If you want floating, spas and silence, read the [Dead Sea hotels guide](/dead-sea-hotels-guide). If you want a city with sea, [Tel Aviv](/best-hotels-tel-aviv) has better restaurants and a cooler summer. Eilat wins on winter sun, on the reef, and on being the gateway to Jordan.
