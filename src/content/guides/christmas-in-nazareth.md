Nazareth keeps Christmas more completely than any other city in Israel. The city's Arab-Christian community — predominantly Melkite Catholic, Greek Orthodox, and Maronite — celebrates Catholic, Orthodox, and Armenian Christmas across six weeks from mid-December to late January. The result is a layered, genuinely local festival season with a character entirely different from the more internationally managed events in Bethlehem or the quieter observances in Jerusalem.

The Christmas season opens on December 12 with the tree lighting at Mary's Well Square and runs through a week-long market, a Christmas Eve parade on December 24, and Mass at the Basilica of the Annunciation on December 25 — then continues into the Orthodox and Armenian seasons in January. Nazareth is also Israel's largest Arab city, with a living Old City and an excellent restaurant and café culture that makes it worth a multi-day stay, not just a day trip.

## Christmas Tree lighting — December 12

The season opens with the Christmas Tree lighting at [Mary's Well Square](/nazareth-travel-guide), the focal point of Nazareth's Old City. Over 30,000 lights are installed around the square and the surrounding streets each year; the ceremony brings the mayor, church leaders, choirs, and tens of thousands of spectators together in the early evening for a countdown and the moment the square floods with light.

No ticket is required — the ceremony is free and the square is public. Arrive by 17:30–18:00 to secure a view along the central walkway. The lighting itself is typically followed by live music and a market warm-up with open stalls; it is the busiest single evening of the pre-Christmas period.

## Nazareth Christmas Market — December 12–19

For the week following the tree lighting, Mary's Well Square becomes an open-air market. Stalls line the square and extend into the Old City streets, selling:

- Local Arab-Christian food — knafeh (the city's famous cheese pastry), sfeeha (lamb flatbread), musakhan (caramelised-onion chicken), and sweets from century-old family confectioners
- Handicrafts, ceramics, olive-wood carvings, and embroidery produced by Galilee artisans
- Christmas decorations, candles, and gifts

Multiple stages run throughout the day and evening with live performances: Arabic choral music, children's school ensembles, and folk music. A Santa Claus photography booth is set up for families. Evening hours (18:00–22:00) draw the largest crowds; daytime visits from 11:00–16:00 are considerably calmer and easier to navigate.

Entry is free. The market closes December 19; the Old City's Christmas lights remain illuminated through the holiday season and beyond.

## Christmas Eve parade — December 24

On December 24 at 15:30, a Christmas Eve procession departs from the Tomb of the Virgin Mary (Tomb of the Virgin / Latin Cemetery area) and moves south along Paul VI Street through the centre of the Old City. The procession includes the Latin Patriarchate delegation, church choirs in vestments, scouts carrying national and church flags, municipal officials, and a large public contingent.

The route along Paul VI Street toward the Basilica of the Annunciation complex is the best vantage point — arrive before 15:00 and claim a spot on the raised footpaths above the street level for a clear view over the procession. Traffic is closed on the route from early afternoon.

Fireworks launch from the city centre at approximately 17:15, visible across Nazareth from the hillside viewpoints above the Old City.

## Church of the Annunciation — December 25

The Basilica of the Annunciation — the largest church in the Middle East, built over the traditional site of the Angel Gabriel's announcement to Mary — holds its main Christmas Masses on December 25:

- **07:00** — Early morning Mass, smaller and more contemplative
- **10:00** — Solemn High Mass, the main festive service; larger congregation, full choir

The 10:00 Mass is the one to attend if you want the full liturgical experience. Arrive 30–45 minutes early: the basilica is large (capacity for thousands) but fills quickly on Christmas morning. Dress modestly — covered shoulders and knees; this is not a tourist visit but an active act of worship attended by the local community.

Entry to the basilica is free year-round. The lower level of the church preserves the Grotto of the Annunciation — a first-century dwelling traditionally identified as Mary's home — and is open for visits outside Mass hours. See the [Nazareth travel guide](/nazareth-travel-guide) for full visiting information on the basilica and the nearby Church of St Joseph.

## Orthodox and Armenian Christmas — January

Nazareth's Christmas season does not end on December 25. The city celebrates two further Christmases, attended primarily by the local community but open to respectful visitors:

- **Greek Orthodox Christmas: January 6–7, 2027.** The Greek Orthodox Church of the Annunciation, set in the Old City opposite the basilica, holds its main festive liturgy on January 7. Services are in Greek and Arabic; the atmosphere is intimate, local, and unhurried — a contrast to the busier December events.
- **Armenian Christmas: January 18–19, 2027.** The Armenian Christian community in Nazareth is smaller, and the January 18–19 celebration is a quieter, community-focused observance.

Visitors attending Orthodox or Armenian services should arrive early, dress modestly, observe quietly, and avoid photography during the liturgy unless explicitly permitted. No ticket is needed for either service.

## Practical information

**Getting there.** From Tel Aviv, bus 343 (Egged/Nateev Express) runs from Tel Aviv Central Bus Station to Nazareth approximately every 30–60 minutes (1 hour 30 minutes). From Jerusalem, change at Afula (approximately 2 hours total). By car from Tel Aviv: 1.5 hours via Route 65; from Jerusalem: 2 hours via Route 6 and Route 65.

**Parking.** The municipal car parks off Ha-Atzma'ut Street and Paulus Street are the closest to Mary's Well and the Old City. On December 24 and December 25, Nazareth's Old City streets are closed to non-resident traffic from early afternoon — arrive before 14:00 or use the peripheral car parks and walk 15 minutes.

**Hotels.** Nazareth's Christmas week (December 19–25) is the city's peak accommodation period. The boutique hotels and guesthouses in the Christian Quarter and around the Old City are the closest and most atmospheric options; they sell out weeks in advance for December 24–25.
