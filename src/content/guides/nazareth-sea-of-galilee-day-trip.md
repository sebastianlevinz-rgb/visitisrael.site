The Galilee is the landscape of the Gospels: the lake, the fishing villages and the green hills where, by tradition, Jesus spent most of his ministry. Within a 30 km arc of the western shore sit Capernaum, Tabgha, the Mount of Beatitudes and Magdala, and Nazareth lies 30 minutes to the west. A Christian day trip from [Tel Aviv](/tel-aviv) or [Jerusalem](/jerusalem) threads them into a single circuit, and it is one of the most booked guided days in the country. This guide covers what you see, what tours include, what they cost, and how to do the same loop by car if you prefer.

## The circuit

Most tours follow a version of this route, in this order.

**Nazareth.** The largest Arab city in Israel and the town of the Annunciation. The **Basilica of the Annunciation**, completed in 1969, is the largest church in the Middle East and stands over the grotto traditionally identified as Mary's home; the courtyard is lined with mosaics of the Madonna donated by Catholic communities around the world. Most tours also pass the Greek Orthodox Church of St Gabriel at Mary's Well and give a few minutes in the old-city market. Some add **Cana**, 10 km away, associated with the wedding miracle.

**Capernaum.** The lakeside village that all four Gospels call Jesus's own town. The white limestone **synagogue** of the 4th or 5th century stands on the black basalt foundations of an earlier one, and a few steps away a modern octagonal church hovers over the excavated **house of Peter**, with a glass floor to see the remains below. This is a national park with an entry fee; allow an hour.

**Mount of Beatitudes.** On the hillside above the lake, the octagonal church built in 1938 marks the traditional site of the Sermon on the Mount. Most visitors spend longer in the gardens than inside: the view across the water to the Golan is the best on the circuit.

**Tabgha.** Two churches on the shore: the **Church of the Multiplication**, with its Byzantine mosaic of the loaves and fishes in front of the altar, and the **Church of the Primacy of Peter**, a small chapel built around a rock on the water's edge. Both are free.

**Yardenit.** Where the Jordan River leaves the lake at its southern end, a baptism site run by Kibbutz Kinneret with changing rooms, robe rental and steps into the river. Pilgrim groups are often baptised or renew their vows here; other visitors simply watch.

Some itineraries add a **wooden-boat crossing** on the lake, a stop at **Magdala**, where a first-century synagogue was discovered in 2009, or time on the **Tiberias** waterfront. Read the itinerary before booking, because two tours with the same name can differ in exactly these extras.

**Group day tours** are the mainstream choice and the only practical option without a car, because the lakeside sites are poorly served by public transport. Expect a good deal of driving, several short walks, and churches that range from ancient and quiet to modern and large. Pilgrim groups often sing or hold short services at the Mount of Beatitudes and Yardenit, which adds to the atmosphere. Mornings are calmer; by midday the lakeside car parks fill with coaches.

**Overnighting** in Tiberias or Nazareth is the honest recommendation if the Galilee matters to you. The drive from either city is two to two and a half hours each way, and a day tour spends nearly half its time on the bus. With a night by the lake you can be at Capernaum when it opens, see Nazareth's old city at an unhurried pace and add Magdala or Cana. See the [Galilee region guide](/galilee) for where to stay.

**Private guides** are common on this circuit because Christian pilgrims often want a specific denominational emphasis, unhurried prayer stops and a guide who knows the biblical geography in depth. Priced per vehicle, they become competitive for a family or small church group.

## What it costs

As of 2026, group day tours from Tel Aviv or Jerusalem generally cost between US$90 and US$130 per person, with transport and a licensed guide included. Most of the Christian sites are free, which is a pleasant contrast with the desert national parks; Capernaum charges a modest entry fee, and Yardenit charges for robe and towel rental if you go into the water. Lunch is normally extra, and the lakeside restaurants near Tabgha and in Tiberias serve the local tilapia, sold as St Peter's fish. A boat crossing, where offered, is sometimes included and sometimes an optional add-on.

## Doing it by car

The Galilee is one of the most rewarding self-drive regions in Israel. From Tiberias, drive north on Route 90 along the western shore to Capernaum first, because it is the northernmost point and the first to fill up; then retrace south through Tabgha and the turn-off up to the Mount of Beatitudes, and finish at Magdala just north of Tiberias. The loop is about 30 km and takes four to five hours with stops. All sites have free car parks, though the small Tabgha car park fills in high season. Nazareth is a 30-minute drive west of the lake on Route 77. For the practicalities of renting, see our [car rental guide](/car-rental-israel).

Arrive at Capernaum before 9 am between May and October to beat the first coaches. The Church of the Multiplication closes for a few hours around midday on Fridays; check current hours before building your day around it.

## Practical details

**Dress.** Shoulders and knees covered at every church, for men and women. A light scarf does the job.

**Heat.** The lakeshore is about 200 metres below sea level. From June to September, temperatures reach 38 to 40°C; start early, carry two litres of water per person and use the churches, which stay cool, for the middle of the day. See [best time to visit Israel](/best-time-to-visit-israel).

**Cash.** Capernaum takes cards at the ticket office. Small restaurants near Tabgha and in the Nazareth market often prefer cash.

**Photography.** Exteriors and gardens are fine everywhere; some chapels restrict interior photography, and the Franciscans ask for silence inside the Church of the Beatitudes.

**Combining.** From Tel Aviv, the Galilee circuit and the [north-coast day of Caesarea, Haifa and Akko](/caesarea-haifa-akko-day-trip) are the two classic northern excursions; with a night in Tiberias you can do both back to back. [Haifa](/haifa) is an hour from the lake.

## Is it worth it?

For anyone with a Christian interest, and for many secular visitors too, yes. Few places let you walk the actual landscape of the Gospels in a single day, and the Galilee is beautiful in its own right, especially in spring. The honest caveats are the amount of bus time on a day tour from either city and the fact that modern churches over ancient sites will not move everyone. If you can spare the night, stay by the lake.
