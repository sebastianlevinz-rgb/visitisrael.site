Tel Aviv does Sukkot its own way. While Jerusalem fills with pilgrims and the Western Wall plaza draws tens of thousands for the Birkat Kohanim at dawn, Tel Aviv throws a week-long outdoor party: live concerts in Hayarkon Park, family gatherings in neighbourhood squares, restaurant terraces built into wooden sukkot, and the Mediterranean at 27°C for anyone who wants it.

The result is one of the most enjoyable weeks of the Israeli calendar — warm, social, and unmistakably Israeli. Sukkot 2026 runs from sunset on **Friday, September 25** through nightfall on **Friday, October 2**, with five full Chol HaMoed days in between where the city operates normally and the outdoor programme is at its peak.

## Sukkot 2026 dates in Tel Aviv

| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| First evening (Yom Tov begins) | Friday, September 25 at sunset |
| First day — Yom Tov (also Shabbat) | Saturday, September 26 |
| Chol HaMoed begins | Sunday, September 27 |
| Last Chol HaMoed day | Thursday, October 1 |
| Hoshana Raba | Friday, October 2 |
| Shemini Atzeret / Simchat Torah | Saturday, October 3 |

The first day (September 26) coincides with Shabbat in 2026 — a double closure that means Tel Aviv restaurants and most shops will be shut, and the light rail runs on a reduced Shabbat timetable. From Sunday, September 27, the city is fully open and the Chol HaMoed activity begins in earnest.

## Hayarkon Park concerts

**Hayarkon Park** (Park HaYarkon) is the heart of Tel Aviv's Sukkot entertainment. At 3,000 dunams, it is Israel's largest urban park — and during Chol HaMoed, its concert stages draw tens of thousands of Israelis for an evening lineup that typically includes tribute acts and Israeli pop headliners.

Past Sukkot programmes at the park have included tribute performances — Elton John, Led Zeppelin, Fleetwood Mac — alongside Israeli chart acts on multiple stages. Free daytime programming typically runs through the Chol HaMoed afternoons (family picnic areas, children's activities, outdoor sports); evening concerts are ticketed.

**Practical information:**

- The main concert area is the Yarkon Amphitheatre (Amphitheater HaYarkon), accessible from the Rokach Boulevard entrance
- Ticketed evening shows often sell out 2–3 weeks before Sukkot; check Leaan.co.il and IsraTicket for listings once the programme is confirmed (usually announced mid-August)
- Public transport: light rail Red Line to Shaul HaMelech station (15-minute walk to park south entrance) or bus 89 from central Tel Aviv
- Parking at the park is extremely limited on Chol HaMoed; arriving by public transport or bicycle is strongly recommended
- Daytime free entry to the park; confirm ticketed show times and pricing at the park's official site (parkyarkon.co.il)

## Dizengoff Square and neighbourhood celebrations

**Dizengoff Square** (Kikar Dizengoff) is Tel Aviv's classic public gathering point, and during Sukkot it hosts family-oriented outdoor programming: dancing, children's entertainment, and community sukkah visiting. The circular plaza and its famous fountain make a natural stage for the informal, open-air celebrations that characterise Tel Aviv's holiday atmosphere.

Beyond Dizengoff, the city's neighbourhood parks and courtyards fill with family gatherings throughout Chol HaMoed. Apartment buildings with communal courtyards often build a shared sukkah; city-owned sukkahs appear in Rabin Square, Meir Park (Gan Meir), and the beach promenade. The lulav-and-etrog market on Carmel Street (south of the Carmel Market) runs in the final days before Sukkot — it is a vivid and photogenic spectacle even for visitors with no intention of performing the ritual.

## Tel Aviv beaches during Sukkot

One of Tel Aviv's most compelling Sukkot advantages over Jerusalem is the weather. Late September on the Mediterranean brings:

- **Water temperature**: 26–28°C — among the warmest of the year
- **Air temperature**: 27–30°C daytime, 21–23°C evenings
- **Sea state**: typically calm with gentle waves; ideal for swimming
- **Lifeguards**: on duty at all main beaches through October

Gordon Beach, Frishman Beach, and the promenade from Tel Aviv Port south to Jaffa are all fully operational during Sukkot week. The Chol HaMoed afternoons (especially Sunday–Tuesday) bring Israeli families to the beach in large numbers; arriving before 10:00am or after 4:00pm avoids the peak crowding. Hilton Beach (dog-friendly, LGBTQ+-welcoming) tends to be slightly quieter than the central beaches.

The combination of beach swimming, Hayarkon Park concerts, and restaurant sukkot means Tel Aviv visitors rarely feel they are "missing" anything by not being in Jerusalem for Sukkot.

## Restaurant sukkot and food

Tel Aviv's restaurant scene adapts well to Sukkot. Many restaurants build a sukkah on their terrace or sidewalk — some simple wooden frames, others elaborately decorated — and seat guests inside throughout Chol HaMoed. Eating inside a sukkah is part of the holiday, and Tel Aviv restaurants lean into the spectacle.

**Best neighbourhoods for sukkah dining:**

- **Rothschild Boulevard**: the tree-lined boulevard has several restaurants with pavement sukkot; the atmosphere on a warm September evening is excellent
- **Neve Tzedek**: boutique restaurants in the neighbourhood's stone lanes build intimate sukkot that fill quickly in the evenings
- **Florentin**: the hipster neighbourhood's bars and cafés tend toward more casual sukkah setups; good for late-night meals
- **Carmel Market area**: the market's surrounding streets have a cluster of mid-range restaurants popular with Israeli families

**Practical note on closures:** Jewish-owned restaurants and most food shops close on the first day (September 26) and on Shemini Atzeret/Simchat Torah (October 3). Stock up on groceries before those closures or head to the beach promenade kiosks (which may operate). The rest of the week, Tel Aviv's restaurant scene operates normally with extended hours.

## The International Storytelling Festival — Givatayim

A short trip from central Tel Aviv (15 minutes by bus or car), the city of **Givatayim** hosts its annual **International Storytelling Festival** during the Sukkot Chol HaMoed period. The festival brings storytellers from Israel and abroad to perform in Hebrew, English, Arabic, and other languages — a distinctive cultural option alongside the concert and beach programme.

The festival takes place at the Givatayim Theatre and outdoor stages in the city centre. Check the festival's website or the Givatayim municipality portal closer to September 2026 for programme details and ticketing; many sessions are suitable for families with children and some are specifically in English.

## Where to stay in Tel Aviv for Sukkot

Tel Aviv's accommodation for Sukkot fills quickly, with beachfront hotels going first. Strategic options by area:

**Beachfront (Herbert Samuel corridor)**: the Hilton, Carlton, and Isrotel Tower are closest to the beach and the promenade — ideal for beach-first Sukkot stays. These sell out earliest and carry the biggest holiday premium.

**Rothschild / Neve Tzedek**: boutique hotels with excellent restaurant and nightlife access. A 10–15 minute walk to the beach or a quick light rail hop to the beach stations. Good value relative to beachfront.

**Florentin / Allenby**: the most budget-friendly option with a strong neighbourhood atmosphere. 20 minutes on foot or 5 minutes by bike to the beach. Suits younger travellers and those who want local neighbourhood life over resort-style proximity.

**Tel Aviv Port area**: quiet and upscale, 5 minutes' walk from Hayarkon Park's north end. Good choice if the park concerts are the priority.

## Getting around Tel Aviv during Sukkot

Tel Aviv's transport situation during Sukkot is more straightforward than Jerusalem's:

- **Light rail Red Line**: operates throughout the holiday week, with reduced frequency on September 26 (Yom Tov + Shabbat) and October 3 (Shemini Atzeret + Shabbat). Normal service resumes each Sunday
- **Buses**: Dan and Egged bus lines run Chol HaMoed days normally; Shabbat-reduced on September 26 and October 3
- **Bicycles**: Tel-O-Fun bike-share stations are fully operational throughout the holiday week — the beach promenade and Rothschild Boulevard bike lane are excellent Sukkot routes
- **Taxis/rideshare**: Gett and Yango operate throughout the week including on Yom Tov closures
- **Inter-city buses**: the Central Bus Station on Levinsky Street runs normal Chol HaMoed schedules for Jerusalem, Haifa, and Beersheba connections
