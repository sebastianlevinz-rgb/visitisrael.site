## Entry requirements: ETA-IL for Thai passport holders

Thai citizens are on Israel's visa-exempt list — **you do not need a full embassy visa**. However, since January 2025, all visa-exempt nationals (including Thai citizens) must obtain an **Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA-IL)** before travelling to Israel.

### Applying for the ETA-IL

- **Apply at:** [iaa.gov.il](https://iaa.gov.il) — the official Israeli Airports Authority portal
- **Fee:** ₪25 (approximately ฿250–270 at recent exchange rates; fee is charged in shekels)
- **Validity:** 2 years, multiple entries
- **Processing time:** typically 72 hours to several days — **apply at least one week before departure**
- **What you need:** your passport number, date of birth and basic travel details — no supporting documents are required for the ETA-IL itself

The ETA-IL is not a guarantee of entry. Israeli immigration officers at Ben Gurion Airport may ask about your travel purpose, accommodation details and return flight. Having your hotel booking confirmation and return ticket accessible on your phone or printed is recommended.

### On arrival at Ben Gurion Airport

Immigration queues at Ben Gurion can be long — allow extra time if arriving during a busy period. Passport stamps: Israel generally does not stamp passports; you will receive a small paper entry card instead. Keep this card for the duration of your stay, as you may be asked to present it at checkpoints or hotels.

For pilgrimage groups: travelling with an organisation's letter of invitation or a tour operator's itinerary is helpful to present at immigration alongside your passport and ETA-IL confirmation.

## Flights from Thailand to Israel

El Al operates the largest direct connection between Thailand and Israel, with approximately **16 weekly flights** in summer 2026 serving both **Bangkok Suvarnabhumi (BKK)** and **Phuket (HKT)**. Israir Airlines has also added Bangkok and Phuket routes (launched for Passover 2026 and continuing).

| Route | Carrier | Approximate Flight Time |
|---|---|---|
| Bangkok (BKK) → Tel Aviv (TLV) direct | El Al, Israir | ~9–10 hours |
| Phuket (HKT) → Tel Aviv (TLV) direct | El Al, Israir | ~10–11 hours |
| Bangkok → Tel Aviv via Dubai (DXB) | FlyDubai | ~13–15 hours |
| Bangkok → Tel Aviv via Doha (DOH) | Qatar Airways | ~13–15 hours |
| Bangkok → Tel Aviv via Istanbul (IST) | Turkish Airlines | ~13–15 hours |

**Note on Emirates:** Emirates has permanently suspended its Tel Aviv service. For connections via Dubai, use FlyDubai (a separate airline from Emirates, also Dubai government-owned).

### When to book

Direct El Al and Israir fares from Bangkok to Tel Aviv are typically most competitive in January–February and October–November. Peak pricing windows coincide with Israel's Jewish holidays and summer: Passover/Songkran (March–April), Jewish High Holidays (September–October) and July–August. Book 2–4 months ahead for best availability on direct routes. See the [cheap flights to Israel guide](/cheap-flights-to-israel) for comparison tools.

## Christian pilgrimage for Thai travellers

Thailand's Christian communities — particularly Thai Catholics in Bangkok, Chiang Rai and Chiang Mai, and Protestant communities associated with the Church of Christ in Thailand (CCT) — organise regular pilgrimage tours to the Holy Land. Israel contains the most significant sites in Christian tradition, compressed into a geography that can be visited in a single trip of 7–12 days.

### The core Holy Land pilgrimage circuit

**Galilee Region — Nazareth:**
- **Basilica of the Annunciation** — the largest church in the Middle East, built over the site where the angel Gabriel is said to have appeared to Mary; the Lower Church features international Marian art donated by countries from around the world
- **Church of the Multiplication of Loaves and Fishes (Tabgha)** — on the shore of the Sea of Galilee; remarkable 5th-century mosaic floor
- **Church of the Beatitudes** — hilltop chapel at the traditional site of the Sermon on the Mount; panoramic views over the Sea of Galilee
- **Capernaum** — ruins of the village where Jesus based his Galilean ministry

**Sea of Galilee:**
- Boat tours operate from Tiberias and Ginosar; during the voyage, guides read the calming-of-the-storm passage; pre-book through your tour operator

**Jordan River — Yardenit Baptism Site:**
- The most popular baptism renewal site in Israel, at the southern outflow of the Sea of Galilee
- White robes available for hire; the site has changing facilities, lockers and a bookshop
- Pilgrims may renew baptismal vows or receive full immersion
- **Note:** the river at Yardenit is an organised visitor site — the Baptism Site of Jesus at Qasr al-Yahud (near Jericho) is a historically distinct location; many itineraries include both

**Jerusalem:**
- **Via Dolorosa (14 Stations of the Cross)** — the route through the Muslim Quarter and Old City to Golgotha; Franciscan friars lead a public procession every Friday (times vary — confirm on arrival); see the [Via Dolorosa guide](/via-dolorosa-guide) for all 14 stations
- **Church of the Holy Sepulchre** — site of the crucifixion, burial and resurrection according to Christian tradition; no entry fee; arrive before 8am or after 5pm to avoid the peak crowds
- **Garden of Gethsemane** — the ancient olive grove where Jesus prayed before his arrest; the Church of All Nations stands adjacent

**Bethlehem (West Bank):**
- **Church of the Nativity** — birthplace of Jesus; UNESCO World Heritage Site; located in the Palestinian Authority, about 10 km south of Jerusalem; most pilgrimage tours include Bethlehem as a half-day trip from Jerusalem; bring your passport for the checkpoint crossing
- The checkpoint at Rachel's Crossing (Checkpoint 300) is straightforward for international tourists with valid passports and a return flight booking

**Other significant sites:**
- **Yad Vashem (Holocaust Memorial and Museum)** — free entry; widely included in pilgrimage itineraries; the Children's Memorial and Hall of Names are particularly moving
- **Mount of Olives** — panoramic viewpoint over the Old City; the Church of the Pater Noster (Lord's Prayer inscribed in 140+ languages); Dominus Flevit church

### Practical pilgrimage tips for Thai groups

- **Modest dress** is required at all churches, the Western Wall and religious sites — covered shoulders and knees for everyone; carry a light scarf or shawl
- **Group size:** most Thai Catholic pilgrimage groups travel with a licensed Israeli Ministry of Tourism guide and a Thai-speaking tour leader
- **Language:** Israeli guides speak English; Thai-language interpretation is sometimes arranged through Thai-run Israel tour operators in Bangkok — check with your travel agent
- For individual travellers not joining an organised group, GetYourGuide offers guided Holy Land day tours with English-speaking licensed Israeli guides from Jerusalem and Tel Aviv

See the full [Christian pilgrimage guide](/christian-pilgrimage-holy-land) for detailed site-by-site logistics.

## Thai agricultural workers in Israel

Israel has one of the largest Thai worker communities in the Middle East — approximately 25,000–30,000 Thai agricultural workers are employed across Israeli kibbutzim and moshavim, particularly in the Galilee, Jordan Valley and Negev. Thai workers in Israeli agriculture represent one of the largest single foreign-worker groups in the sector.

Family members visiting Thai workers should note:

- **Accommodation:** Thai workers typically live in employer-provided kibbutz or moshav accommodation; plan for your own hotel or guesthouse if you are visiting independently
- **Transport:** many agricultural communities are not on major public transport routes — plan to rent a car or arrange private transport; see the [car rental guide](/car-rental-israel) for practical information
- **Royal Thai Embassy Tel Aviv:** the Embassy of Thailand in Israel is located in Tel Aviv and can provide consular assistance, passport renewal and emergency support; verify the current address at mfa.go.th/tel-aviv before travel

## Payments and banking

**PromptPay does NOT work in Israel.** Israeli payment terminal infrastructure does not support PromptPay or any Thai digital payment system.

### What works in Israel

| Method | Works? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Visa / Mastercard (KBank, Bangkok Bank, SCB, Krungthai, TMBThanachart) | ✓ Yes | Widely accepted; inform your bank before travel |
| Apple Pay / Google Pay (linked to Visa or Mastercard) | ✓ Yes | Works on NFC-enabled terminals |
| PromptPay | ✗ No | Not supported in Israel |
| Thai Baht (THB) cash | ✗ No | Not exchanged in Israel |

### Cash tips

- Withdraw **New Israeli Shekel (₪)** from Israeli ATMs using your Visa or Mastercard — select "charge in local currency (NIS)" to avoid dynamic currency conversion fees
- Carry approximately ₪200–400 in cash for markets, street food, sheruts (shared taxis) and small shops
- **Thai Baht cannot be exchanged in Israel.** Convert to USD or EUR before departure in Bangkok or at Suvarnabhumi Airport, or withdraw NIS directly from Israeli ATMs on arrival using your international Visa or Mastercard
- For the current THB/ILS exchange rate, check xe.com — this guide does not quote a fixed rate

## Health insurance

Thai government health schemes — including the Universal Coverage Scheme (Gold Card / NHSO), the Social Security Scheme (SSO) and the Civil Servant Medical Benefit Scheme (CSMBS) — **do not cover medical costs outside Thailand**. An emergency room visit or hospital stay in Israel for an uninsured tourist can be costly.

Take out an international travel insurance policy before departure. Confirm that **Israel is explicitly listed as a covered destination** in the policy wording.

International providers with Israel coverage widely used by Thai travellers: SafetyWing Nomad Insurance, World Nomads, Allianz Travel, AXA Travel Insurance. Thai insurers such as Muang Thai Insurance and Bangkok Insurance also offer international travel plans — verify Israel coverage before purchasing.

## Power adapter

Israel uses **Type H sockets** — a unique three-flat-pin format found almost nowhere else in the world.

Thailand uses **Type A/B sockets** (US-style flat pins) or **Type C** (round pins on older fittings). Thai plugs do not fit Israeli sockets without an adapter.

**Voltage is compatible:** Thailand operates at 220V/50Hz and Israel at 230V/50Hz. Most modern phones, laptop chargers and camera chargers are marked "100–240V" and work with only a **plug adapter** (no voltage converter needed). Check the label on each device before travel. Type A/B-to-Type H adapters are available at MBK Center or Chatuchak Weekend Market in Bangkok and are considerably cheaper than adapters available at Ben Gurion Airport.

## Mobile connectivity

Thai SIM cards (AIS, DTAC/True, NBTC) work in Israel on roaming. Roaming rates can be high — compare your carrier's international roaming packs before departure.

Better options for connectivity in Israel:

1. **Israeli tourist SIM** — available at Ben Gurion Airport arrivals hall from Golan Telecom and Hot Mobile kiosks (typically open around the clock). A 30-day unlimited-data SIM costs approximately ₪50–90; your Thai phone must be SIM-unlocked to use it
2. **eSIM** — providers such as Airalo, Nomad and Holafly offer Israel data eSIMs; activate before leaving Thailand on compatible handsets (iPhone XS or later, many Android devices)

## Planning your Israel trip from Thailand

A 10–12 day itinerary covers the main pilgrimage and sightseeing circuit comfortably: **2–3 nights Jerusalem** (Old City, Via Dolorosa, Church of the Holy Sepulchre, Garden of Gethsemane, Yad Vashem, Mount of Olives); **a Galilee overnight or day trip** (Basilica of the Annunciation in Nazareth, Sea of Galilee boat, Yardenit Jordan River baptism); **a Bethlehem morning** (Church of the Nativity, Manger Square); and **1–2 nights Tel Aviv** (Jaffa Old City, Mediterranean beaches, Carmel Market).
