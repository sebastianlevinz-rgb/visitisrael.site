Bethlehem, the birthplace of Jesus in Christian tradition, lies only 10 km south of [Jerusalem](/jerusalem), but it sits on the other side of the separation barrier in the West Bank. That single fact shapes the logistics: you cross a checkpoint, you carry your passport, and you leave the rental car behind. Done with a little preparation, it is a straightforward and moving half-day, and one of the most booked excursions from Jerusalem. This guide explains what you will see, how the tour formats differ, how the crossing works and what to bring.

## What you will see

The centrepiece is the **Church of the Nativity**, one of the oldest continuously used churches in the world. Constantine built the first basilica here around 330 CE and Justinian rebuilt it in the 6th century; parts of the original mosaic floor are visible through trapdoors in the nave. Below the altar, reached by a narrow staircase, is the **Grotto of the Nativity**, where a fourteen-pointed silver star marks the traditional birthplace. The church is shared by the Greek Orthodox, Roman Catholic and Armenian communities under a centuries-old arrangement that governs everything down to who cleans which column.

Around it:

- **Manger Square**, the plaza in front of the basilica, with the Mosque of Omar on the far side and cafés and taxis in between.
- **St Catherine's Church**, the Franciscan church next door where the Christmas Eve Midnight Mass is celebrated.
- **The Milk Grotto**, a small chalk-white chapel a short walk away, calm and often skipped by rushed groups.
- **Shepherds' Field** in neighbouring Beit Sahour, with two chapels commemorating the announcement to the shepherds; usually included only on longer tours.
- **The olive-wood workshops**, where nativity sets and mother-of-pearl work have been carved for generations.

Many tours also drive along a section of the separation barrier to see the street art painted on it, including several works attributed to Banksy.

### The half-day tour

The standard product. A coach or minibus collects you from your Jerusalem hotel mid-morning, drives 30 to 40 minutes south through the checkpoint and gives you about three hours in Bethlehem: the basilica, the Grotto, Manger Square, the Milk Grotto and usually a stop at an olive-wood workshop. You are back in Jerusalem by early afternoon with time for the Old City. Half-day groups tend to be larger and the workshop stop can feel long; if that bothers you, look for a smaller group or a full-day format. Tours are widely available with English-speaking guides, and Civitatis lists departures led in Spanish, with other languages on some dates.

### Bethlehem, Jericho and the Jordan River

The fuller day heads south to Bethlehem first, then east and down more than a thousand metres through the Judean Desert to **Jericho**, one of the oldest continuously inhabited settlements on earth at around 250 metres below sea level. Stops typically include the mound of ancient Jericho (Tell es-Sultan), the cable car up to the Greek Orthodox **Monastery of the Temptation** on the cliff above the town, and the ancient sycamore associated with the story of Zacchaeus. Most versions finish at **Qasr al-Yahud**, the baptism site on the Jordan River where pilgrims enter the water in white robes. It is a long day, roughly 8 am to 7 pm, but the change of landscape is dramatic and the two destinations do not repeat each other. Masada cannot realistically be added to this itinerary; see the [Masada visitor guide](/masada-visitor-guide) for that separate day.

### Going independently

Arab bus 231 leaves from the bus station opposite Damascus Gate and drops you near Checkpoint 300 in about 20 to 30 minutes. You walk through the checkpoint building, and local taxis wait on the far side for the five-minute ride to Manger Square; agree the fare or ask for the meter first. Reverse the process to return. This route is cheap and reliable and suits travellers who want to spend an unhurried morning at their own pace. What you lose is the guide's explanation of a church whose layers, from Constantine to the Crusaders to the present-day sharing arrangement, are genuinely hard to read on your own.

## Crossing into the West Bank

This is the part first-timers worry about, and it is simpler than it sounds.

- **The checkpoint.** Bethlehem is in Area A, administered by the Palestinian Authority. Tourists enter and leave through an Israeli crossing at the separation barrier. Guided coaches usually pass with a brief document check; on foot, expect an X-ray for bags, a passport check and a metal detector. It typically takes a few minutes, longer on Friday mornings and in December.
- **Passport.** Mandatory, and physical. No stamp is added for tourists.
- **Rental cars.** Standard Israeli rental agreements exclude Area A, so driving your hire car to Bethlehem voids the insurance. Park in Jerusalem and use a tour, the bus or a licensed taxi. Our [car rental guide](/car-rental-israel) has the detail.
- **Photography.** Do not photograph inside the checkpoint building. In Bethlehem itself, photography is welcome; ask before photographing people.
- **Money.** Israeli shekels are accepted everywhere; US dollars and euros are widely taken in shops. Cards are less reliable than in Jerusalem, so carry some cash for lunch, the workshops and tips.

For visa and entry questions about Israel itself, see [visa information](/visa-information). For the broader safety picture, see [is Israel safe](/is-israel-safe).

## Practical details

**How long does it take?** The drive is 25 to 40 minutes each way plus the checkpoint. A half-day tour is four to five hours door to door; the Jericho combination nine to eleven.

**What to bring.** Passport, modest clothing for the churches, comfortable shoes for stone streets and the Grotto steps, water and sun protection, and cash. If you plan to enter the water at the Jordan River, a towel and a change of clothes; white robes can be rented on site.

**Queues at the Grotto.** Twenty to forty minutes is normal at midday. Tours that reach the church before 9 am, or independent visitors who arrive after 4 pm, wait far less.

**Christmas.** Manger Square is decorated through December and the Latin Midnight Mass on 24 December is ticketed months ahead through the Latin Patriarchate. Book tours and Jerusalem hotels early for that period; see [best hotels in Jerusalem](/best-hotels-jerusalem).

**Ramadan and Shabbat.** Ramadan changes some restaurant hours in Bethlehem but does not close the sites. Shabbat does not affect Bethlehem, which makes it a useful Saturday excursion when much of West Jerusalem is closed, though bus 231 and tour departures still run.

## Is it worth it?

For anyone spending two or more days in Jerusalem, yes. The Church of the Nativity is one of the genuine highlights of the Holy Land, the crossing is far less daunting than the forums suggest, and the contrast between a 4th-century basilica and a working Palestinian town gives the morning real texture. The honest caveat is that the cheapest half-day tours are large and shop-heavy. If Bethlehem matters to you, spend a little more on a smaller group or add Jericho.
