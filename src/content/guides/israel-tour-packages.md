A multi-day package bundles the parts of an Israel trip that take the most planning: hotels, a licensed guide, transport between cities and the headline sights, in one booking with a fixed itinerary. It is the format most first-time visitors and most pilgrimage groups choose, and it removes the two things that complicate independent travel here, driving and Shabbat. This guide explains the standard package lengths, what they include, how small-group, private and coach formats compare, what to expect to pay, and how to decide whether a package suits you at all.

## Who packages suit

- **First-time visitors** who want the essentials handled and the history explained.
- **Pilgrims** following the Christian sites across Jerusalem, Bethlehem, Nazareth and the Galilee, where a structured itinerary with time for prayer matters.
- **Travellers who would rather not drive**, or who do not want to work out the Friday-to-Saturday transport gap themselves.
- **Anyone combining Israel and Jordan**, because the operator handles the Wadi Araba crossing in both directions.

Packages suit independent travellers less well: the pace is set, evenings are often in hotels chosen for group capacity rather than character, and Tel Aviv, which rewards wandering, tends to get a single afternoon. If that describes you, our [7-day](/itineraries/7-days-in-israel) and [10-day](/itineraries/10-days-in-israel) itineraries are built for doing the same circuit on your own, with day tours booked where they add value.

## Package lengths compared

| Length | Typical route | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **2–4 days** | Jerusalem Old City, Bethlehem, Masada and the Dead Sea | Add-on to a Tel Aviv stay or a cruise stop |
| **5–6 days** | The above plus Tel Aviv and Jaffa, and a Galilee day | A short first visit |
| **7–8 days** | Jerusalem, Bethlehem, Dead Sea, Caesarea and Haifa, Nazareth and Galilee, Tel Aviv | The classic circuit; most operators' flagship |
| **10–12 days** | The classic circuit plus Eilat and the Negev, or Petra and Wadi Rum | Longer trips; Jordan combinations |
| **13+ days** | Israel, Jordan and sometimes Egypt | Regional grand tours |

### Two to four days

Short packages are usually based in Jerusalem and are sold either to travellers already in Tel Aviv or to cruise passengers arriving at Haifa or Ashdod. A standard three-day version gives a full day in the Old City, a [Bethlehem half day](/jerusalem-bethlehem-day-trip) with the afternoon at Yad Vashem or the Mount of Olives, and the [Masada and Dead Sea day](/masada-visitor-guide). They are efficient and reasonably priced, and they can be assembled almost identically from three separate day tours if you prefer to choose your own hotel.

### Seven to eight days

The classic. Most operators have refined this circuit over years, and it runs roughly: arrival and an orientation walk in [Tel Aviv](/tel-aviv); two days in [Jerusalem](/jerusalem) covering the Old City, Yad Vashem and the Mount of Olives; Bethlehem; Masada and the [Dead Sea](/dead-sea); the drive north with stops at Caesarea and Haifa's Bahá'í Gardens; a full [Galilee day](/nazareth-sea-of-galilee-day-trip) from Tiberias with Nazareth, Capernaum and the Mount of Beatitudes; and a return to Tel Aviv. It sees everything a first visit should, wastes little time on the road, and is the format that pilgrimage operators and general-interest operators both sell, with different emphasis.

### Ten to twelve days

The longer packages add one of two extensions. The **southern** one continues from the Dead Sea into the [Negev](/negev), with the Ramon Crater and often a night in a desert lodge or Bedouin camp, and finishes in [Eilat](/eilat) for the reef. The **Jordan** one crosses from Eilat for [Petra](/petra-from-israel), Wadi Rum and sometimes Amman and Jerash, with the operator managing the border, the Jordanian guide and the return. If Petra is on your list, the combined package is the least stressful way to get there.

## Small group, private or coach

| Format | Group size | Character | Price band (per person, 7–8 days) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Coach tour** | 30–50 | Fixed itinerary; pilgrimage standard | Lowest |
| **Small-group tour** | 8–16 | Licensed guide, minibus, some free time | Middle |
| **Private tour** | 1–8 | Your own guide, vehicle and pace | Highest per person, competitive for four or more |

**Coach tours** are how most church groups travel, and they are the cheapest way to see the circuit with a guide. The compromise is time: 40 people take a long while to get through the Church of the Holy Sepulchre or onto a Galilee boat.

**Small-group tours** cap at somewhere between 8 and 16, though some operators stretch the term to 20; ask for the confirmed maximum. They fit into the Western Wall tunnels and Akko's underground, leave evenings free, and are the natural choice for couples and solo travellers. The international small-group brands run Israel and Israel-plus-Jordan departures on set dates, and there are faith-focused and scholar-led versions with a Christian, Jewish-heritage or archaeological emphasis.

**Private tours** are priced per vehicle and guide rather than per person, so a family of four or a group of six often pays little more per head than a small-group departure, with complete control of the route and pace. They are also the only format that adapts to a specific denominational focus or a special interest such as wine or archaeology.

## What is included, and what is not

- **Included almost everywhere:** hotels with breakfast, transport, a Ministry of Tourism licensed guide, and the core sights.
- **Sometimes included:** entrance fees, dinners, the Sea of Galilee boat, the Masada cable car, airport transfers. Read the list.
- **Rarely included:** lunches, tips for guide and driver, travel insurance, the Jordanian border fees on combined tours, single supplements.

Two packages with similar headline prices can differ by a dozen meals and several entrance fees. Compare the inclusions before comparing the price.

## What packages cost

As of 2026, two to three day packages generally cost between US$600 and US$1,100 per person; seven to eight day guided circuits with three or four star hotels between US$1,800 and US$4,000, with the range driven mainly by hotel category and group size; and ten to twelve day Israel and Jordan combinations from around US$3,000 upward. Solo travellers pay a single supplement unless the operator matches roommates. Prices peak at Easter, in October and at Christmas.

## Comparing prices: normalise the airfare first

Published package prices are not comparable as they stand, and almost no operator says so. Reading the pilgrimage catalogues on 23 September 2026, we found headline prices that included the international flight, headline prices that began at Ben Gurion Airport, and headline prices that excluded every flight, including the one from the operator's own departure city. Two "eight-day Holy Land" packages can differ by the cost of a long-haul ticket before you have compared a single hotel.

Work out which of these four formats a price belongs to before you compare anything else.

| Price format | Where your trip starts | What the headline price normally covers | What you still have to buy | Chaplain and daily mass |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Air-inclusive, European departure** | Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia or Málaga | Return flight, hotels, most meals, guide, ground transport | Tips, single supplement, some lunches | **Yes** on the parish-linked programmes; **no** on the general-tourism ones |
| **Air-inclusive, American departure** | A New York or home-city gateway; Mexico City or Bogotá for Latin American operators | Return flight, hotels, half board, guide, ground transport | Tips in most cases, lunches, single supplement | **No** on the large Protestant programmes; **yes** on the Mexican and Colombian Catholic ones |
| **Land only** | Ben Gurion Airport, where the group assembles | Hotels, ground transport, guide, half or full board | **The entire international flight**, plus tips unless stated otherwise | **Yes** on the Franciscan programmes, which also prepay tips; **no** on the Protestant ones |
| **Ground only, flights sold separately** | Your own home airport, on your own booking | Hotels, ground transport, guide | Every flight, including the home-country leg that some operators leave out | **Only from a minimum group size** on at least one Spanish operator: 20 pilgrims |

**What the ranges looked like in September 2026.** Air-inclusive eight-day departures from Spain published starting prices roughly between 1,500 and 2,800 euros per person. Air-inclusive departures from North America for the nine or ten day standard clustered around US$3,000 to US$4,000, while Latin American operators ran from about US$1,400 for a short circuit to over US$5,000 for a two-week one. Land-only programmes of seven to eleven days, which begin at the airport in Israel, ran from about US$1,400 to US$3,700. Those are published "from" prices read on 23 September 2026, not quotes; the operator's own page is the only current number.

### The hidden costs: tips, taxes and supplements

**Tips are the line that catches people out.** They are expected for the guide and the driver on every itinerary, and operators handle them in three different ways: built into the price, charged as a fixed prepaid amount, or left entirely to you. One American pilgrimage operator publishes the figure openly — Pilgrim Tours declares prepaid tips of **US$110 to US$140 per person** for the trip, as a separate line (pilgrimtours.com, consulted 23 September 2026). Franciscan Pilgrimage Programs and 206 Tours include tips in the package price; Educational Opportunities includes only the hotel staff. A package that looks US$100 cheaper may simply have moved that line off the headline.

**The other separated lines.** Italian operators are the most explicit about this: Brevivet lists a registration fee of €39, airport taxes of €230 and a single-room supplement of €360 as separate items alongside the tour price (brevivet.it, consulted 23 September 2026). Operators whose itineraries cross into Jordan or Egypt usually declare the border fees the same way. None of it is hidden in the dishonest sense; it is simply not in the number on the brochure cover, which is why the comparison has to be done on the inclusions.

### Chaplain and daily mass: the line that splits the market

Whether a priest travels with the group, and whether mass is celebrated every day, is the biggest single difference between two packages of the same length and price — and it is not always on the product page. Of the catalogues read on 23 September 2026:

- **Stated in writing:** 206 Tours and Franciscan Pilgrimage Programs in the United States, Halcón Viajes in Spain (a Franciscan guide, with accommodation in Franciscan houses), San Juan del Hospital in Valencia and Peregrinaciones.mx in Mexico.
- **Not mentioned at all:** the large Protestant and evangelical programmes, and several general-tourism operators selling in Spanish. The silence is deliberate, because those itineraries are built for a different kind of group.
- **Only from a minimum group size:** Éxodo Peregrinaciones assigns a chaplain **only from 20 pilgrims**; Peregrino.travel promises mass "when possible", coordinated with the Custody of the Holy Land.

The itinerary gives it away faster than the sales copy does. If daily mass is the point of your trip, ask for it in writing before you pay a deposit, and ask who books the slots at the shrines: in Jerusalem those are allocated centrally rather than arranged by the guide on the morning. Our [Christian sites in Israel](/christian-sites-israel) guide sets out which sites the pilgrimage itineraries actually visit and how the traditions differ as a matter of routing.

## Booking and practicalities

**Book early** for spring and autumn departures, which sell out six to twelve months ahead on the well-known small-group brands; summer and mid-winter have more availability.

**Visas.** Every traveller applies individually; there is no group visa for Israel. Most visitors need an ETA-IL before flying. See [visa information](/visa-information).

**Shabbat.** Ask how the itinerary handles Friday afternoon to Saturday evening. Well-designed packages put the Dead Sea, the Galilee or an Arab-majority city such as Nazareth or Akko on Saturday, where everything is open.

**Hotels.** Group hotels in Jerusalem are often outside the Old City walls and in Tiberias rather than by the lake. If a particular neighbourhood matters, check the hotel list before booking.

**Insurance and phone.** Packages cover transport but not insurance or connectivity; arrange both before you fly. Our [first time in Israel](/first-time-in-israel) guide covers the basics.

**Pace.** Expect early starts, most days on the road by 8 am, and a great deal of history in a short time. Build a free day into your trip before or after the package if you can.

## Package or independent?

Take the package if you want the guide, the pilgrimage structure, or the Jordan crossing handled. Go independent, with day tours where they earn their place, if you want to choose your own hotels, linger in Tel Aviv and set your own pace; Israel is a small country with good roads and trains, and our [car rental guide](/car-rental-israel) covers the driving side. A common compromise is a three-day Jerusalem package plus independent days in Tel Aviv and the north, which gets the guided context where it matters most.
