Combining Israel, Jordan and Egypt in a single journey is one of the most ambitious and rewarding itineraries in the world: ancient walled cities, the world's most famous archaeological ruins, the Dead Sea, Petra's rose-red rock, the Sinai coast, and the Pyramids of Giza — all within a few hundred kilometres of each other. This guide explains the route options, border crossing logistics, realistic time requirements and sample itinerary frameworks for 10-, 14- and 21-day trips.

If you are only combining two of the three countries, see our dedicated guides: [Israel and Jordan 10-day itinerary](/israel-jordan-itinerary) and [Israel and Egypt combined trip](/israel-egypt-guide). For a single-day Petra excursion from Israel, see [Petra from Israel](/petra-from-israel).

**Safety note:** All three countries have distinct travel advisories that change regularly. Before booking, check your government's current advice: travel.state.gov (US), gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice (UK), smartraveller.gov.au (Australia). This guide is for planning purposes; it cannot replace real-time government advisories.

## The two canonical routes

### Route A: Israel-first (most common)

1. **Fly into Tel Aviv** (Ben Gurion Airport — the most connected hub in the region for Western flights).
2. **Israel** — Jerusalem, Dead Sea, Masada, Eilat (southward progression).
3. **Cross to Jordan** at the Wadi Araba/Yitzhak Rabin crossing (Eilat → Aqaba).
4. **Jordan** — Aqaba, Wadi Rum, Petra, Amman.
5. **Cross to Egypt** — either fly Amman → Cairo (typically 1.5 hours, several daily flights), or take the Aqaba–Nuweiba ferry across the Gulf of Aqaba to the Sinai coast, then travel overland.
6. **Egypt** — Cairo, Pyramids of Giza, Grand Egyptian Museum; optional Luxor extension.
7. **Fly home from Cairo** (or fly Cairo → Tel Aviv and exit via Ben Gurion if your itinerary ends there).

Israel-first is the most popular structure because Ben Gurion has better international flight connections than Amman or Aqaba, and the southward progression from Jerusalem to the Dead Sea to Masada to Eilat is a logical geographic flow.

### Route B: Egypt-first (reverse)

1. **Fly into Cairo** — explore the Pyramids and the Grand Egyptian Museum first.
2. **Cross to Sinai** via overnight train or bus + Taba/Eilat crossing.
3. **Enter Israel at Eilat** and travel north: Masada, Dead Sea, Jerusalem, Galilee, Tel Aviv.
4. **Cross to Jordan** at the Wadi Araba crossing from Eilat → Aqaba (or via the Allenby Bridge between Jerusalem and Amman for a different entry point into Jordan).
5. **Jordan** — Petra, Wadi Rum, Amman.
6. **Fly home from Amman**.

Egypt-first suits travellers whose home-country flights are better priced or routed via Cairo, or those who want to build toward the spiritual intensity of Jerusalem as a closing experience.

## Border crossings explained

### Eilat–Aqaba (Wadi Araba crossing)

The **Wadi Araba crossing** (Israeli side: Yitzhak Rabin/Arava Peace Crossing; Jordanian side: Wadi Araba) is the most tourist-friendly of Israel's three Jordan crossings. Located about 4 km from Eilat city centre, it is open daily — including Shabbat, when the northern Allenby Bridge has more restricted hours. Processing typically takes 45 minutes to 2 hours; allow more time on busy weekends or Israeli public holidays.

- **Israel exit fee**: approximately ₪101 (~$27) — cash or card; verify current amount at the terminal.
- **Jordan entry**: present passport + Jordan Pass or purchase visa on arrival (~$55/JD 40 equivalent).
- **Transport to the crossing from Eilat**: taxi (~₪30–50 from city centre, 10 minutes).

See our [border crossings guide](/border-crossings) for full procedures and current hours.

### Aqaba–Nuweiba ferry (Jordan to Egypt overland)

The **Aqaba–Nuweiba ferry** (operated by Arab Bridge Maritime) crosses the Gulf of Aqaba between Aqaba port and Nuweiba on the Sinai Peninsula. The fast ferry takes approximately 90 minutes; the conventional (slow) ferry takes 3–4 hours. Schedules are seasonal and subject to change — book in advance at the Arab Bridge Maritime office or through a local operator in Aqaba.

Nuweiba is the entry point to South Sinai. From there you can travel south to Dahab or Sharm el-Sheikh by shared taxi or minibus, or connect by road toward Cairo (a long journey — consider flying instead for the Cairo leg).

**Important:** The Sinai Peninsula has an active travel advisory from most Western governments. South Sinai tourist areas (Dahab, Sharm el-Sheikh, Ras Mohammed) are typically assessed separately from North Sinai in government advisories. Check your government's current guidance for South Sinai specifically before planning the ferry route.

### Taba–Eilat (Egypt to Israel, or Israel to Egypt)

The **Taba crossing** on the Red Sea coast (Israeli side: Eilat International Border Terminal; Egyptian side: Taba) is the land crossing used if you are combining the two countries by road rather than flying. It is a 9 km taxi ride from Eilat city centre. See our full [Israel and Egypt combined trip guide](/israel-egypt-guide) for detailed Taba crossing procedures.

### Amman to Cairo by air

For most 14-day travellers, **flying from Amman (AMM) to Cairo (CAI)** is the cleanest way to connect Jordan and Egypt. Royal Jordanian operates multiple daily flights; journey time is approximately 1.5 hours. This avoids the long overland route through Sinai and removes the ferry variable from your itinerary.

## Practical notes

**Money:** You will handle three currencies — Israeli Shekels (₪), Jordanian Dinars (JD), and Egyptian Pounds (EGP). USD cash is useful as a backup at borders. Bring sufficient USD cash for visa fees (Egypt: ~$25–35; Jordan on-arrival: ~$55). ATMs are reliable in cities in all three countries but less accessible near border crossings. Wise or Revolut cards reduce foreign-exchange fees across all three currencies.

**Jordan Pass:** If you are spending more than one night in Jordan and visiting three or more Jordan Tourism Board sites, the Jordan Pass typically saves money by bundling the visa fee with site entry fees including Petra. Buy it online at jordanpass.jo before reaching the Wadi Araba crossing.

**ETA-IL (Israel):** If flying into Ben Gurion, Israel's Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA-IL) is required for most Western passport holders. Apply at iaa.gov.il at least 72 hours before departure. The fee is ₪25 (~$7). It covers re-entry, so one ETA-IL covers the full trip if you exit and re-enter Israel as part of a loop. See [visa information](/visa-information) for eligibility.

**Safety:** All three countries have travel advisories that are updated in real time. Egypt's Sinai Peninsula and Jordan's border regions adjacent to Syria in the far north have historically carried elevated advisory levels for certain areas. Cairo, central Jordan (Amman, Petra, Aqaba), and all standard tourist areas of Israel are generally accessible, but **always check [is-israel-safe](/is-israel-safe) and your home government's advisories for Egypt and Jordan before finalising plans.**

**Flying vs overland for Cairo:** Most travellers find it worth the flight cost to fly Amman → Cairo rather than doing the overland Sinai route, which is longer, more complex, and subject to Sinai-specific advisories. The flight is typically 1.5 hours and avoids the ferry and road variables entirely.
